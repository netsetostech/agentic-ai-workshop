Interview Q&A: the ingest lane, box by box

Ten questions an interviewer asks a production engineer about an event-driven ingest pipeline on Google Cloud, answered the way a senior engineer answers them: the mechanism, the failure it prevents, the number, and what you would say if pushed.

Interviewers probing this topic are rarely testing whether you know Pub/Sub. They are testing whether you can place every box, say what crosses each arrow, and predict what a reader sees when the box in the middle dies. The good answers name the resource type a log line lands under, the HTTP code a handler returns and why, the write order and its crash semantics, and the one number that decides whether a message reaches the dead-letter queue. DocuMind's lane is the worked example throughout; the mechanisms transfer to any bucket-to-index pipeline.

Conceptual · Q1

Why a Cloud Storage notification into a Pub/Sub push subscription, rather than polling the bucket or an Eventarc trigger straight to the service?

Polling is out because it costs a listing per interval and still misses nothing only if you also keep a cursor; a notification is one event per finished write, carrying the object record with its generation, and deletes cost nothing because the nightly walk finds them. The push subscription is the piece that carries the operational guarantees: at-least-once delivery with a ceiling (max_delivery_attempts = 12, then a dead-letter topic), an exponential backoff from 10 s to 600 s, an ack deadline of 600 s that matches the service's request timeout, and an OIDC token minted as the worker's own service account, so a service that admits no unauthenticated caller is reachable by Pub/Sub and nothing else. An Eventarc trigger delivers a CloudEvent straight to the service, around the subscription - around its token, its retry ceiling and its DLQ - and DocuMind's earlier draft did exactly that and answered 400 to every event because the worker parses a Pub/Sub envelope. If pushed: the trigger is five resources in one file, eventarc.tf, and two IAM grants that fail silently when missing - the storage agent's publisher on the topic and the Pub/Sub agent's tokenCreator on the worker's account.

Conceptual · Q2

Pub/Sub only understands 2xx versus everything else. Why does the handler still use three status codes, and why are a stale event and a duplicate answered 200?

The rule is one line: a 2xx acknowledges, anything else is a nack that walks the retry policy to the DLQ. So the handler's job is to decide which outcomes are finished and which are worth another attempt. A stale event (an older generation than the ledger holds), a duplicate (the claim lost to an earlier delivery), a queued document, a withdrawn source, an undo - each is a finished state that a retry could only repeat, so each is a 200, and each leaves its own log line. A 500 is reserved for a failure the next attempt may fix, and the claim is released first, so the retry can take it; otherwise "give the claim back" gives nothing back and the document stays claimed for ever with every delivery acked. The 400 for an unparseable message is not for Pub/Sub - it retries a 400 exactly as it retries a 500 - it is for the people and the pager: ingest_poison is not ingest_failed, and the failure alert reads the metric that counts the latter and not the former. If pushed: the DLQ therefore holds two kinds of message, poison and persistently failing, and deliveryAttempt is twelve on both.

Conceptual · Q3

Walk me through the write order - Firestore staged, the swap, Vector Search, the mirrors, the ledger - and tell me what a reader sees if the worker dies between any two writes.

The rows land in Firestore first with current=false and staged=true, so nothing is visible; the swap flips the new version current in one pass and then retires the old by flag, so at no instant is there no version and at no instant are two whole versions offered; the ANN upsert follows the swap, so an index id never points at a row that is not current; the managed mirror and the BigQuery mirror come next, and neither can fail the ingest; the claim, the ledger row and the fingerprint come last, and only then the audit event and the ingest_ok line. Die before the swap: a staged version with a one-day expire_at, deleted by the TTL policy, and the retry re-stages the same ids. Die after the swap but before the upsert: Firestore is whole, the ANN tier catches up on the retry or on make backfill-vectors, and the Firestore rung answers meanwhile. Die before the ledger row: the reader already sees the new version, and the nightly walk records the generation as a touch. If pushed: the reader's newest-per-source guard is what makes the swap's two-commit window on a long document invisible; the write order and that guard are one design.

Practical · Q4

A tenant uploaded a document ten minutes ago and it is not searchable. Where do you look, in what order?

First the worker's log for that tenant and window, no event filter: gcloud logging read 'resource.labels.service_name="documind-ingest" AND jsonPayload.tenant="acme" AND timestamp>=...'. An ingest_ok line means it is indexed - then make vector-status, because a streamed upsert takes minutes to show in vectorsCount, and the answer's found_by tells you which rung served. An ingest_queued_batch line means it was over 250 pages: read consumer on the line, make queued, and either wait for :15 or make batch. An ingest_duplicate or ingest_already_current means it was already there under the same bytes - the search problem is elsewhere. An ingest_failed names the exception; the claim is failed, the message is retrying towards the DLQ, and make dlq after the twelfth attempt shows it. No line at all: the object is at the bucket root or under the wrong prefix (poison, a 400 you would have seen), the notification's publisher grant is missing, or the subscription points at another revision's URL - gcloud storage buckets notifications list and gcloud pubsub subscriptions describe. If pushed: the claim document documents/{tenant}_{sha} holds the same status and error text as the log.

Practical · Q5

The dead-letter subscription shows a message with deliveryAttempt: 12 for an object named invoice.pdf with no folder. What happened, and what do you do?

The first folder of the object path is the tenant; an object at the bucket root has none, so IngestMessage refused it with "object is not under a tenant prefix", the worker answered 400 and logged ingest_poison, and Pub/Sub redelivered it eleven more times with backoff before forwarding it to the DLQ - roughly an hour after the upload. Nothing was downloaded, nothing was billed. The fix is not on the lane: copy the object to acme/invoice.pdf (a new object, a new event, the normal path), then acknowledge the dead-letter message with gcloud pubsub subscriptions pull ingest-dlq-sub --auto-ack so it stops reappearing in make dlq, which peeks without acking. If pushed: the same shape with a valid path and a zero-byte body fails size >= 1; and the reason it is twelve and not five is that a corpus upload of thirty objects against a concurrency-1 worker burned five attempts on cold-start refusals that were never the document's fault.

Practical · Q6

After a bad deploy of the worker, which environment variables do you check first, and what does each one break?

AUDIT_BUCKET first, because the container refuses to start without it - by design, so a configuration mistake does not appear as a 500 halfway through an ingest that then retries into the DLQ. Then DOCAI_PROCESSOR_ID and RESIDENCY: a missing id fails every PDF at _parse, and a residency that differs from the one Terraform created the processor with calls a processor in the wrong location. VECTOR_INDEX_NAME empty skips the ANN tier silently - the ingest succeeds, Firestore is whole, and every answer says found_by: firestore - which is why make smoke refuses a deployment on vector that answered from the rung. EMBEDDING_MODEL and EMBEDDING_VERSION must equal the API's pair and the stamp on the rows, or the carry-over reuses nothing and every re-issue embeds everything. RETENTION_DAYS must equal the value the TTL policy was reasoned with, or the undo window and the deletion disagree. BATCH_JOB empty is not an error, but the line says nothing is started when a document is queued. If pushed: make deploy-services reads the first five out of the Terraform state precisely so that a human never types them.

Practical · Q7

You ran the reconcile by hand from Cloud Shell every night for a week and the drift alert never fired. Was the lane healthy?

You do not know, and the alert could not have told you. make reconcile APPLY=1 runs reconcile.py in your shell; its reconcile_done line is printed to your terminal and never reaches Cloud Logging. The documind/reconcile_drift metric reads resource.type="cloud_run_job" with job_name="documind-reconcile" - the Cloud Run job's stdout - and the two reconcile policies are only declared when RECONCILE_JOB=true was on the plan. So the number existed, on your screen, and the pager watched an empty series. Make it observable by declaring the job (make build, then make reconcile-job, an apply with the switch on) and letting the 23:30 IST schedule run it, or by executing it (gcloud run jobs execute documind-reconcile --wait) when you want a run now. If pushed: the policy fires on drift above zero for 24 hours - two nightly runs - because one night's drift is a lost event the walk just repaired, and two is a lane nobody is reconciling.

System design · Q8

Design the path for a 1,000-page document without adding a second service or a second queue.

Count the pages before paying for anything: pypdf reads the page tree for free, Document AI is roughly a second a page in 15-page online slices, and 600 s of request budget therefore fits about 250 pages. Over that, the handler writes a claim document - ingest_batch/{doc_key} with the object, its generation and its type, and documents/{doc_key} set to queued - and answers 200; the queue is a Firestore query for status == queued, oldest first, so there is no second Pub/Sub topic to provision. The consumer is a Cloud Run job on the very same image with the very same environment, timeout 7200s, max_retries 0, calling the worker's own index_document(lane="batch") with the bytes fetched by generation; it takes each claim queued to processing in a transaction, so two runs never index one document twice. It is started by the worker as it queues, through the Jobs API as the worker's own identity, and by an hourly schedule regardless, so a document is indexed minutes later and not at the next hour. A redelivery of the original event meets a queued claim and is acked as such; the nightly walk reports the generation as queued, not drift. If pushed: a failed document stays failed with its reason and the job never retakes it - a re-upload or make reindex is the way back.

System design · Q9

Design the trail: a failure pages within ten minutes, a poison object does not, and the night's drift is a number someone can chart.

One JSON line per event with event as the first field and the tenant on every line, so the query language is one filter. Three log-based metrics: a counter of the lifecycle events labelled by event and tenant, restricted by a regex to the five that matter (ok, superseded, reactivated, stale_event, failed), and two distributions of embedded and reused off every ingest_ok, so "pay for what changed" is a chart. A policy on the counter with event="ingest_failed", summed per tenant over 600 s, threshold zero, with a documentation block that says what to run. A fourth metric on the job's reconcile_done.drift under cloud_run_job, and a policy that fires only when the p99 stays above zero for 86,400 s, plus one on Cloud Run's own failed-task-attempt count so a job that never finishes is also a page. The poison line stays out of the regex on purpose: it is a log search, and the answer to it is a human reading make dlq. If pushed: I would add ingest_poison and ingest_queued_batch to the counter under their own labels for the chart, and leave the failure policy's filter on ingest_failed alone.

Trade-off · Q10

Concurrency 1 and thirty instances, or concurrency 8 and four instances, for this worker?

Concurrency 1. A request on this service holds a document claim while it parses, scans and embeds, sometimes for minutes; a second request on the same instance contends for the two vCPUs and stretches the first one's time towards the 600 s ack deadline, and Pub/Sub redelivers whatever is not acked in time - one slow instance turning one document into three deliveries and three claims, two of them refused as duplicates after doing the download. The cost of concurrency 1 is a fleet of small instances during a corpus load, and Cloud Run's cold-start refusals while that fleet forms are exactly why the retry ceiling is twelve. The cost of concurrency 8 would be a smaller fleet and cheaper idle - except the lane idles at zero instances anyway, so there is nothing to save at night, and the price of a lost claim is a document that is silently absent. If pushed: I would revisit the number only after moving the heavy work out of the request - the batch job already runs the same pipeline with no deadline, and a design where every document went that way could raise concurrency on the handler because the handler would then only claim and enqueue.

How to prepare

Draw the lane from memory once a day for a week - bucket, notification, topic, subscription, worker, stores, jobs, DLQ - and write the number on each arrow.

Run make ingest-one and make poison on a lane and read every line they leave with gcloud logging read, then find the same facts in eventarc.tf and alerts.tf.

For every write in step 5 of lesson 3.2, be able to say what the reader sees if the worker dies right after it.
