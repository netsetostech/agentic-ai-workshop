Live Session: the lane on the whiteboard, then live

Ninety minutes in which the room draws the ingest lane box by box, then watches every box answer a gcloud describe, follows one document through its seven writes, sends a poison object towards the dead-letter queue, and reads the trail back from Cloud Logging.

Session goal

By the end, every learner can draw the lane from the uploads bucket to the Firestore row without notes and name the Terraform file behind each box. They can find any of the worker's log lines with one gcloud logging read and say which resource type a job's line lands under. And they can explain, with the numbers, why a poison object takes twelve visits to reach the DLQ and why a 400 and a 500 are the same to Pub/Sub and different to the pager.

Prep checklist
  • The lane from Module 2 is up on your demo project (make up done, make smoke green), make batch-job and make reconcile-job applied once so the job lists are not empty.
  • Cloud Shell open with PROJECT, TENANT=acme, REGION exported and cd code; a second tab on the Firestore console at the chunks collection; a third on the Logs Explorer.
  • The log filter pasted and ready: resource.type="cloud_run_revision" AND resource.labels.service_name="documind-ingest" AND jsonPayload.event=~"^(ingest_|ledger_)".
  • make poison run about an hour before the session, so the DLQ has a message to show at minute 60; a fresh one goes in during the session for the first refusal.
  • Lesson 3.2 open at step 1 (the lanes diagram) and step 4 (the spine); terraform/eventarc.tf and commands/lesson-12.5.sh open in an editor.

The trigger, on the whiteboard (5-20)

Draw six boxes left to right and label the arrows with what crosses them: the object record (JSON_API_V1), the Pub/Sub message, the HTTPS POST with an OIDC token, the 200 or the nack. Then open the file and read the subscription resource. The line to point at is max_delivery_attempts = 12, and the question for the room: the README says five - which one is applied, and how would you prove it? (The describe in the next segment.)

bash - Cloud Shell
grep -n 'max_delivery_attempts\|ack_deadline_seconds\|minimum_backoff\|maximum_backoff\|event_types\|payload_format' terraform/eventarc.tf
grep -n 'five' README.md terraform/eventarc.tf Makefile | head

The wiring, live (20-35)

bash - Cloud Shell
gcloud storage buckets notifications list gs://$PROJECT-uploads
gcloud pubsub subscriptions describe documind-ingest-push --project $PROJECT --format='yaml(ackDeadlineSeconds,deadLetterPolicy,pushConfig,retryPolicy)'
gcloud run services describe documind-ingest --region $REGION --project $PROJECT --format='yaml(spec.template.spec.containerConcurrency,spec.template.spec.timeoutSeconds,spec.template.spec.containers[0].env)'
↔ SWIPE

Point at maxDeliveryAttempts: 12 and at ackDeadlineSeconds: 600 beside timeoutSeconds: 600. Ask: what happens to a document that takes 601 seconds? (Redelivered mid-flight, a second claim refused as a duplicate - which is why the batch lane exists.) Then scroll the env list and ask which variable would stop the container from starting at all (AUDIT_BUCKET).

One document, seven writes (35-55)

bash - Cloud Shell
SINCE=$(date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ)
make ingest-one FILE=evals/demo/gratuity_amendment_2026.md TENANT=$TENANT PROJECT=$PROJECT
gcloud logging read "resource.type=\"cloud_run_revision\" AND resource.labels.service_name=\"documind-ingest\" AND jsonPayload.event=~\"^(ingest_ok|ingest_superseded|ledger_fingerprint)$\" AND timestamp>=\"$SINCE\"" --project $PROJECT --limit 5 --format=json
make sources TENANT_ONLY=$TENANT PROJECT=$PROJECT | grep -i 'gratuity\|ledger'
gcloud storage ls gs://$PROJECT-audit/$(date -u +%Y/%m/%d)/$TENANT/
↔ SWIPE

Point at reused, embedded, retired and effective_from on the line (the amendment declares a date). In the Firestore console show the row's current, chunk_hash, embedding_task_type and the absence of staged. The question: if the worker had died one line before swap_versions, what would the console show, and for how long? (A staged row with expire_at a day out, then nothing.)

Point at the two schedules, at "applied": false on the plan's last line, at the worker's 400 with the pydantic error, and at deliveryAttempt on the hour-old message. Ask the room to predict the number before you show it, and to say why the fresh poison is not there yet.

The reader and the trail (70-80)

Show the ledger line of make sources twice, before and after the ingest of minute 35, and tell the story of cache_stale in one sentence. Then open alerts.tf at the ingest_events filter and ask: which of today's lines are in it? (ingest_ok and ingest_superseded; not ingest_poison, not ingest_duplicate.)

If it breaks: no ingest_ok in five minutes

Read the worker's most recent lines without an event filter: gcloud logging read 'resource.labels.service_name="documind-ingest"' --limit 10. An ingest_duplicate means the demo document was already in from a rehearsal - use make reindex with a changed copy, or a different file. An ingest_failed names the exception on the line; the claim is failed and the next delivery retakes it. No line at all: the push subscription is not pointing at this revision's URL or the invoker grant is missing - gcloud pubsub subscriptions describe and gcloud run services get-iam-policy documind-ingest.

If it breaks: make dlq prints nothing

It is not broken. With twelve attempts and a backoff that doubles from 10 s towards 600 s, the hour-old poison may still be mid-retry. Show the climbing count instead: gcloud logging read '... jsonPayload.event="ingest_poison"' --limit 20 | wc -l, and come back to the DLQ at the end of the session.

If it breaks: the job lists are empty

batch.tf and reconcile.tf create nothing until BATCH_JOB=true and RECONCILE_JOB=true are on a make plan with an image in the state. Say so, show the count = local.batch ? 1 : 0 line, and run the plan from Cloud Shell instead; the job segment becomes the explanation of why the drift metric reads cloud_run_job and a shell run never feeds it.

Discussion prompts

  1. Pub/Sub treats a 400 and a 500 the same. Why does the worker bother to distinguish them?
  2. The worker never deletes and never reads "the object as it is now". Which box on the diagram would break first if either rule were dropped?
  3. The reconcile job's environment has no VECTOR_INDEX_NAME. What does that mean for a retired id in the ANN tier, and who cleans it up?
  4. The bucket deletes objects after a year. What does the ledger look like the morning after, and is that a bug?
  5. Which of the seven writes would you move, and what would a reader see during the new window?
