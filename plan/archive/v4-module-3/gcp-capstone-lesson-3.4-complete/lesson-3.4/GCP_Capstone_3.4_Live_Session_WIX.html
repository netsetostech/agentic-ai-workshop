Live session: the ledger, read and then run

Ninety minutes on the code that keeps one current version per document. The room reads idempotency.py with you, then watches the same functions act on a live lane: a re-issue, the undo, a poison message, the queue and the nightly plan. Every segment ends on a log line the learners can find themselves.

Session goal

By the end, every learner can open claim(), swap_versions() and reactivate() and say what each writes, in what order, and what breaks if the order changed. They have watched the three lines of the lifecycle - ingest_ok, ingest_superseded, ingest_reactivated - appear on a real lane and can read reused, embedded and retired off them. And they can say, in one sentence, why the undo embedded nothing.

Segment 1 - The map (0-5)

Open idempotency.py at line 79 and read the comment block aloud without explaining. Then: "Every sentence here is one function. We read them in this order, then watch them act." Point at the names most learners have not met: stale_generation, current_chunks, reactivate, refresh_fingerprint.

Question to the room: "If you had to delete one sentence of this block, which one, and what breaks the same day?"

Segment 2 - The claim (5-20)

Read claim() (17-51) line by line and stop at retakes: ask what a claim in failed is. Then the notebook's race cell. Ask the room to predict the two counts before you run it.

python — the notebook, the race cell
both_won, one_won = race(lock=None), race(lock=threading.Lock())
print("without a transaction:", both_won, "of 20 trials indexed the document twice")
print("with one:            ", one_won, "of 20 trials indexed it twice")

The line to point at: the first number is not zero - "that is the docstring's window measured in milliseconds, and Pub/Sub delivers duplicates concurrently, so it is hit." Then release() (66-76) and its docstring: the crash between claim and finish that left a document missing with nothing alerting.

Question: "Why is queued never in the retake set, even with retake=True?" (The batch lane owns it; take_batch() is the only thing that moves it.)

Segment 3 - The guard and the other lane (20-30)

Read stale_generation() (162-173) beside contracts.is_stale() (113-121). Have the room call five cases - 5 against 7, 7 against 7, 9 against 7, "x" against 7, 5 against no row - before the table. Then push() 328-333: the download names the generation.

The line to point at: "the generation guard (5 < 7 stale, 7 and 9 not)" in the selftest's output. Then current_chunks() (114-136): "Which lane wrote rows without a claim, and why does the worker not simply trust them?" (The stamp: model, version, RETRIEVAL_DOCUMENT.)

Segment 4 - The swap on a fake Firestore (30-45)

Run the practice lab's exercise 3: the kit's real swap_versions(), reactivate() and retire_previous() against a fake of the Firestore client, rows printed after each call.

python — exercise 3, the calls to watch
gone = led.swap_versions(db, "acme", URI, K2, expire_at=expire(), effective_to="2026-10-01")

The line to point at: the commit counter - a three-chunk swap is one commit. Ask what happens at 400 writes and who hides the moment when both versions are current (retriever.newest_per_source()). Then pop one row from the fake and call reactivate() again: reactivate_incomplete with rows 2, chunks 3.

Question: "Which loop ran first, and what would a reader see if we swapped the loops?"

Segment 6 - push() and the HTTP codes (60-72)

bash — Cloud Shell
make poison PROJECT=$PROJECT TENANT=acme       # a zero-byte object; the worker answers 400 and logs ingest_poison

While the target waits, walk push() 309-419 on the projector: the 400, the guard, the claim, the three refusals (undo, queued_batch, duplicate), current_chunks, index_document, the 500. Draw the three-row table: 200 acks; 400 and 500 are both negative acknowledgements that Pub/Sub retries until max_delivery_attempts (12 in eventarc.tf) and then parks in the DLQ. The difference is what the ledger holds: nothing for a 400, a failed claim with the error text for a 500.

Question: "The comment in push() says a 400 stops the retries. Does it?" (No - Pub/Sub treats any non-2xx as a nack. The 400 buys a line that says poison and no work on each retry.)

Segment 8 - The trace and homework (82-90)

Put the three tables of the main page's step 9 on the projector and fill them with the real doc_keys, generations and fingerprints from segment 5. Ask each learner for the one sentence the module gate requires. Then the homework box.

If it breaks

No worker line within five minutes of the upload

Check the worker and the subscription: gcloud run services describe documind-ingest --region $REGION --project $PROJECT --format='value(status.url)', then gcloud pubsub subscriptions describe documind-ingest-push --project $PROJECT - the push endpoint must be that URL. Read the last lines without an event filter: gcloud logging read 'resource.labels.service_name="documind-ingest"' --limit 10 --project $PROJECT. An ingest_failed line names the error; the same text is on the claim row in documents/.

Version 1 again gives ingest_ok instead of ingest_reactivated

The undo was refused and the bytes were ingested fresh. The reason is on the line before it: gcloud logging read 'jsonPayload.event="reactivate_incomplete"' --limit 1 --project $PROJECT - fewer rows than the claim counted, or a stamp past RETENTION_DAYS. Both are the design working: say so, and run the sequence again from version 2, which now reuses everything.

The same bytes are acked as ingest_withdrawn

A previous session ran make retire on the fixture and never restored it; the tombstone holds against the undo by design. make restore SOURCE=acme/smoke_note.md PROJECT=$PROJECT clears it and rewrites the object; the worker's next line is ingest_reactivated inside the window. Use it as the demonstration of step 6 that it is.

Discussion prompts

  1. The claim is keyed by content hash and the ledger by object path. Name a situation where each key alone gives the wrong answer.
  2. swap_versions() and Vector Search are never in one transaction. What does the worker do instead, and what is the worst a reader can see?
  3. RETENTION_DAYS is one number for the audit window and the undo window. Argue for two numbers, then against.
  4. Why does the worker refuse to start without AUDIT_BUCKET instead of failing on the first document?
  5. The nightly walk re-ingests by rewriting the object onto itself. What would a second ingestion path have cost this codebase?
