Level 0 · Theory

Three figures, three ways to order the work

Decide each step as you go, plan first and replan once, or plan once and never look back.

Three figures in one question. The task set's %%N_CHAINED%% chained rows each ask for three figures: when the notice ends (notice_end, from handbook clause NP-03), how many leave days are encashed (encashable_days, with LV-07's cap), and the gratuity (gratuity_estimate, from the Code on Social Security, 2020, s.53). Each figure is a search for its clause, then its calculator. No figure feeds another calculator: a calculator takes its inputs only from the question, or from the right clause of a passage the turn read. Lesson 10.5 teaches that check, check_args. So a turn makes six tool calls at least, and something has to decide their order.

The loop decides each step. Lesson 10.1's loop is one model call a pass: the model reads the whole turn so far, then calls one tool or answers. It chooses each call after reading the last result. On the row with the manager's cap the bench's loop makes %%LOOP_CALLS%% calls: a search and its calculator, three times over, then the answer. Every call reads every passage found so far, so its last call reads %%LOOP_LAST_IN%% tokens and the turn %%LOOP_IN%% in all, by the kit's script.

Plan-and-execute. A planner call writes the whole plan before any tool runs: numbered steps, one tool call a step. An executor then carries it out a step at a time. Each step is one model call with the desk's tools bound. It reads the question, the plan and what the steps so far returned, and makes the one call its step names; LangGraph's ToolNode runs it, and the step ends on the tool's result. When a step is refused or fails, the steps not yet run go back to the planner, once a turn (MAX_REPLANS = %%MAX_REPLANS%%). A solver call writes the answer from every step's results. Four roles: plan, act, replan, solve.

ReWOO: plan once. ReWOO, reasoning without observation, writes every step in one planner call. A later step uses an earlier step's result by its placeholder: in #E6 = notice_end[{"ack_date": "2026-10-05", "days": "#E4"}], #E4 is the figure an extract step reads from the passages #E1 found. Code checks the whole plan before any tool runs, works out what each step waits for, and runs the plan in waves: every step whose inputs are ready runs together. No model call is made between the tools except the extract worker's, and it must quote the sentence its figure is written in. One solver call answers, and nothing replans. Three roles: plan, extract, solve.

A cap the question states. In the third row the person adds "my manager says the encashment cap is 100 days". LV-07 says 45. check_args takes the cap only from LV-07 of a passage the turn read, so it refuses 100. The loop reads LV-07 before it encashes, so it never offers 100. A plan written before anything is read can take the manager's figure, and the bench's scripted planner does, on purpose. Plan-and-execute then meets a refusal and replans once; ReWOO, which never replans, leaves the encashment unworked.

Roles, and a cheaper executor. The planner's calls are few and read the most; the executor's are many and small. The bench can put the act and extract calls on another model the kit prices (EXEC_MODELS: %%EXEC_MODELS%%). %%LITE%% costs USD %%LITE_IN%% in and %%LITE_OUT%% out a million tokens, against flash's %%RATE_IN%% and %%RATE_OUT%%: a sixth of the input price and a fifth of the output price. The turn's Meter and the recorder price each call at its own model's rates, and tag it with its role.

📋

Three HR offices settle the same exit. In the first, one officer works the file herself. She pulls the notice clause and works out the date, then decides what to fetch next: the leave clause, the encashment, the gratuity section. In the second, a senior officer writes a numbered checklist on the file's cover before anyone opens it, and a junior clerk works it one item at a time, writing each result under its item. The payroll system takes a figure only from the employee's letter or from the handbook, so it refuses the manager's "100 days". The clerk stops and sends the file back to the senior, once, to rewrite the items still to do. In the third office the senior writes every item at once, with blanks to fill such as "the cap from item 2's clause", and goes home. The clerks do every item that waits on nothing at the same time, and a reader copies each figure off its clause and must quote the sentence. Nobody rewrites that list, so a refused item stays undone. In all three offices the senior's hour costs more than a clerk's.

One question, three ways

Choose a chained row and the executor's model, then step through the three turns one card at a time. A card is one model call and what code did with it, or one wave of tools that code runs with no model call at all. The strip under each name is the turn's model calls, one letter a role: L the loop, P plan, A act, E extract, R replan, S solve. The row with the manager's cap comes first.

One chained question, three patterns - commands/agent_patterns.py, one card at a time

Every card is the kit's own turn, run when this page was built: each row's scripted models, the ones make agent-turn OFFLINE=1 uses, over the kit's own corpus. The token counts are the script's: four characters a token, and %%SCRIPT_TH%% thinking tokens a call at %%DEFAULT_LEVEL%%. The flash-lite executor changes no step and no token, only what the act and extract calls cost. Steps 8 and 9 show your lane's own counts.

What the panel is not

The scripted planner writes the same plan every time, and takes the manager's cap on purpose. On your lane Gemini writes its own. It may search LV-07 first, so that no step is refused and no replan shows; it may write a plan code cannot read, which plan-and-execute counts as a failed step and ReWOO refuses; its extract worker may paraphrase where it must quote. What does not change is who does what: the planner writes the steps, code runs and checks them, a calculator works out every figure, and the turn's one Meter counts every role's calls against %%MAX_CALLS%% calls and Rs %%BUDGET%%.
