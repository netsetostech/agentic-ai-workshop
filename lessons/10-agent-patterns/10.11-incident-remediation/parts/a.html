Level 0 · Theory

What incident auto-remediation is, and how a design is scored

Something fails; rules read it and print a plan; a person runs the plan. The desk below runs the kit's own rules on the kit's own recordings.

Incident auto-remediation is two jobs: deciding and doing. When an alert policy pages, or a smoke fails, or someone says the chat is broken, something has to decide what is wrong and what would fix it, and something has to do it. The common sketch gives both jobs to one agent with a model inside: it reads the alert and the logs, names a cause and runs the fix. DocuMind's design splits them. make remediate decides, by rules. It reads one Cloud Run service %%N_READS_W%% times at Rs 0, the service's traffic and template, its revisions, and its own log lines of the last hour, and the first of %%N_RULES_W%% rules that holds names the diagnosis. Then it prints the plan and stops. The doing is a person's: you paste the commands, in order. Of the %%N_RULES_W%% rules, %%N_PLAN_RULES_W%% end in a plan or in nothing to do, and the other %%N_PERSON_RULES_W%% end with a person: when the policy that paged is a person's or is not one the kit declares, when the traffic is split, when no change inside the window explains the failure, or when a newer revision has appeared that the rules cannot tell from a fix.

The fault you will hold is real, and nothing pages for it. make incident-drill HOLD=1 sets CHAT_MODEL=%%TYPO%% on documind-chat, a model name one letter short. The new revision takes the traffic, every agent turn fails, and nothing pages, because no alert policy the kit declares counts a service's 5xx answers. make remediate reads it anyway. It finds the serving revision created inside the window, names the one variable that changed, finds no failure on the revision before it, and prints the rollback to that revision, the verify and the restore. You run the three steps; RESTORE=1 is the drill's own copy of the restore, which also clears the drill's record.

A design is scored the way the capstone is. The capstone rubric, which lesson 11.8 publishes, has %%N_COMPONENTS%% components that must exist in the learner's fork, each checked PASS or FAIL by make capstone-check, and %%N_DIMS%% dimensions the defence scores 0 to 3, %%POINTS%% points in all, with %%PASS%% to pass and no dimension at 0. Four of the components are this page's proof: the architecture, naming at least three rejected options and why (component 5); the cost model in rupees at %%USD_INR%% to the dollar, dated (component 6); four failure modes, each proved by a script (component 7); and a runbook with one recorded rollback (component 8). The last one is the drill: the author's run in step 3 records a rollback taken from the plan this page prints, with remediate's reading of the lane before it and after it.

A station master during a signal failure. The home signal shows nothing. He does not guess, and he does not try the levers to see what happens. He reads his instruments, the track circuits and the train register: what is on the line, and since when. He finds the one rule in the book that fits, writes the authority it calls for, and hands it to the driver, who acts on it. His register lets an inspector replay every decision he made. When nothing in the book fits, two trains in one section or a failure nobody caused, he does not improvise: he calls the control office. And some levers are not his at all; the book keeps them for the signal engineer.

The instruments are remediate's %%N_READS_W%% reads. The rule book is its %%N_RULES_W%% rules, the authority is the printed plan, and the driver is you. The register is a recording: SAVE= keeps what was read, and OFFLINE=1 replays the decision. The control office is "page a person", and the levers kept for someone else are the denylist every printed command passes, and the write commands remediate never runs.

What the desk is not

It is the kit's rules on recorded states, not your lane. The kit's %%N_FIXTURES_W%% recordings were written by hand in the shape SAVE= writes, and no lane was read for them; the %%N_BUILT_W%% built states are the tests' own. The author's run in step 3 saves three states from a real lane, and they join the list once they are recorded. Nothing on the desk calls a model or changes anything, and neither does remediate: it only reads.

Two of these words carry the design. A plan is not an action: remediate's last line says 0 model calls and that nothing on the lane was changed, and that is true by construction, because the only commands it can run are its three reads. And "page a person" is an answer, not a failure. A split traffic, or a failure nothing in the window explains, is exactly the incident a person should see first, with what was read beside it.
