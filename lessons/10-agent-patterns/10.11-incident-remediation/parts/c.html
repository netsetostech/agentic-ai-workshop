Dimension 3 · Cost at 10×

Cost at 10×: what breaks first is the read

Ten times the bill is ten times zero. What stops being linear is the %%LIMIT%% lines remediate reads, and the rules forget a rollback before the window closes.

Definition

The commonest 2 on this dimension scales the bill by ten and stops. Here the bill does not scale at all, so the question is only what stops being linear, and the kit declares it in a few constants. The arithmetic takes the 730-hour month the price pages use and two lines a chat turn, a request line and a chat row, which is what a turn writes on a lane deployed with the defaults.

LimitDeclared inValueWhat it allowsAt 1M turns a month

So what breaks first at 10×? Take the top scale and multiply it: a million chat turns a month is %%PER_HOUR_1M%% turns in an average hour and %%LINES_1M%% lines. remediate reads the newest %%LIMIT%%, which cover the newest %%COVER_1M%% minutes of that hour, and its printout says the counts are theirs. The first reading of a fault still holds, because the serving revision's lines are the newest. The second reading breaks: after the rollback, the failing revision's lines are the oldest in the window, they leave the read within the hour, and the rolled-back lane reads pinned_new, a person's, instead of rolled_back with its restore. A busy hour is worse than the average one. That is the threshold in step 5's third row: past %%LIMIT_TURNS%% turns a month, the counts per revision come from a metric, not from the lines.

The bottom of the range is not linear either. At 1k turns a month an hour holds %%PER_HOUR_1K%% turns on average, and a failure needs %%MIN_BAD_W%% bad lines: a quiet lane can hold a fault for an hour without showing two. That is why the drill runs its smoke once before it stops, and why the plan's verify is a smoke rather than a wait. The router's share policies have the same floor in their own terms: they page only in an hour of %%HOUR_FLOOR%% routed turns or more, which an average hour reaches at %%HOUR_FLOOR_TURNS%% turns a month.

Dimension 4 · Evals

Evals: what the kit's tests hold, and the checks that cannot fail

%%N_TESTS%% tests block a change to the rules; three checks in the design pass by construction, and the page names what stands in for each.

Definition

The kit's gate for remediate is commands/tests/test_remediate.py: %%N_TESTS%% tests, standard library only, nothing reaching Google Cloud. The learner repository's dry-run workflow runs it on every push, in its step Command regressions (offline), so a change that moves a rule turns the build red. %%N_OFFLINE_TESTS%% of the tests need neither bash nor make, and the cell below runs those; the drill's %%N_DRILL_TESTS_W%% run incident-drill.sh under bash against a stub gcloud, and one of the %%N_WIRING_TESTS_W%% wiring tests needs make.

Then the rubric's hardest point: which checks cannot fail. Three in this design are vacuous, each enforced by something upstream:

  • The verify right after the rollback. make smoke-chat passes then because the traffic is pinned to the revision before the fault. It proves the rollback took, and says nothing about the fault, which is still in the template. The plan does not stop there: the restore follows, then the verify again, then remediate, which must read no_failure with the traffic following the latest revision. RESTORE=1 checks that the latest revision takes all of it.
  • No failure in an empty window. A revision that answered nothing cannot fail. remediate says so instead of passing quietly: it prints that the revision answered nothing in the window, and names the smoke that would ask it.
  • The placeholder test, on a recording SAVE= wrote. test_the_fixtures_carry_placeholders_only looks in every fixture for a lane's identifiers and for a person's fields. On a recording SAVE= made, most of it cannot fail: save_state() refuses a state that still holds a lane's identifier, and line_of() never keeps a user, a session or a question. What keeps the test worth having is a fixture written by hand, and its one check nothing upstream makes: no mailbox but the service accounts'.
