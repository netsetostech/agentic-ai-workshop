Module 10 · Agent patterns · Lesson 10.5

Run a desk as an agent with checked calculator arguments: every number from the question or its clause, then three arms on the same rows

In lesson 10.4 the routed Desk sent a question that asks for a figure to a desk running as a small agent. This lesson opens that agent. The model reads the passages and chooses each calculator call; code does the arithmetic. A calculator is only as right as what goes into it, so before one runs, check_args() takes every argument from its one source: a fact about the person from their own message, a handbook figure from its clause of a passage the turn read. The cap a manager mentioned, the 60 days of the notice clause, the 31 of "31 December", the 07 of "LV-07": each is refused, in words the model reads and acts on. You try the check on the kit's own passages, ask the Desk page for a figure and give it a wrong cap, read the agent's code and three of its tests, delete the prompt's rule and watch the check refuse anyway, send one question to two arms over REST, and read what the Desk logs and keeps. Last, you run the Desk's three eval arms on the same %%N_ROWS_W%% rows: the routed Desk, code alone, and one agent with every tool. You set them side by side with a paired difference that says what %%N_ROWS_W%% rows can and cannot show, then combine them: a vote over what code returned, a tie handed to a person, and a mixture-of-agents aggregator whose invented figure the check catches.

Level 0 · Theory

The teller's rule: every number from the slip or the rate card

Where each argument of a calculator may come from, why that is checked in code, and what the check cannot see.

A desk that runs as an agent. In lesson 10.4, when the router's classifier says a question asks for a figure (needs_calculation), the handbook or statute desk does not answer with one rag-api call. It runs as a small agent: services/chat/desk_agent.py, a LangChain create_agent loop on %%MODEL%% at thinking level %%THINKING%%. The agent searches the desk's own documents and calls the desk's calculators from shared/desk_calc.py. The model chooses the searches, the calculator and its arguments. Code does the arithmetic, and code writes the lines under the answer that show the formula and where each number came from.

The arithmetic is not the risk; the arguments are. min(50, 45) is 45 whoever computes it. What can go wrong is what goes in. A person writes "my manager says the encashment cap is 100 days", and a model passes 100. A question asks about encashment and notice at once, the passages hold both clauses, and the model takes NP-03's 60 days as the encashment cap. It reads 31 from "lapses on 31 December", 7 from the clause code "LV-07", 3 from the grade "E3", 5 from the Code's "five years". Or the person gives no balance, and a model fills one in. Every one of those calls would run, and every result would be arithmetic done right on a number nobody wrote where it had to be written.

One source for each argument. desk_calc.CALCULATORS names, for every argument, the one place it may come from. A fact about the person (months, days, a balance, a wage, years, a headcount, a date) must be written in the person's own message, as the router masked it. A handbook figure (the accrual rate and the carry-forward cap in LV-01, the encashment cap in LV-07, the notice days in NP-03, or PB-02 on probation) must be written in that clause of a handbook passage this turn read. A flag such as fixed_term is true only when the message says it, and not just after a "not". check_args() checks each argument against its source before the calculator runs. Anything else is refused, and the refusal is an error result the model reads: "ask them for it", or "search for that clause and use the figure it states". The model can try again with a number that has a source, or say which number is missing.

The clause, not the passage. A passage is a chunk the search returned; a clause is the words from its code, such as LV-07, to the next clause code. A cap must be in LV-07's words, not merely somewhere in what the turn read, so NP-03's 60 never passes for LV-07's cap. check_args() reads digits (40,000 and 1,50,000 alike), number words (fifty, twenty-six), lakh, crore and k. The digits of a date are never a number of their own, and a clause code or a grade is a name, not a number.

A Code's calculator needs the Code. The statute desk's %%N_LAW_CALCS%% calculators (a gratuity estimate, the date wages are due, the grievance committee threshold) take only facts about the person, because a Code's own figures are the same in every company that holds it, and desk_calc fixes them. So a Code's calculator runs only when this turn's passages include that Code's own text, and a company that does not hold the Code gets no figure from it.

🏦

The teller at a bank counter, booking a fixed deposit. The amount and the tenure come from the customer's own slip, in the customer's hand. The interest rate comes from the printed rate card on the counter, and only from the line for that scheme: the senior citizens' line, not the savings line above it. If the customer says "my neighbour got 9 percent", the teller does not key in 9: it is not on the card. If the slip has no amount, the teller asks for one and never guesses. The booking system takes no rate that is not on the card's line, so a quick teller and a careless one book the same deposit. What the system cannot tell is whether the teller read the line for the right scheme: that is what the branch's own checks are for.

What the panel is not

It is not a measure of how often a model brings a wrong number: only a run on a lane can say that, and steps 5 and 8 make one. Nor is the check the whole guard. Of the %%N_ACCEPT%% accepted calls, %%N_EDGE%% pass with a figure the kit's row does not expect. In %%N_EDGE_ROLE%% of them the number is written in the right source but stands for something else. LV-01 writes both the accrual rate and the carry-forward cap, and the check reads %%LV01_NUMS%% in its words, so a cap of 1.75 or a rate of 30 passes: %%EDGE_ACCRUED%%. PB-02 writes six months of probation, three months of extension and 15 days of notice, and the check reads %%PB02_NUMS%%, so notice days of 6 pass: %%EDGE_NOTICE%%. fig-01's message writes "8 months", so years = 8 passes: %%EDGE_YEARS%%. In the other %%N_EDGE_PERSON%% the clause is right for someone else: PB-02's 15 days for a confirmed E3, NP-03's 60 for a person on probation. check_args() proves where a number is written; it cannot know what the number stands for there, or which of two clauses covers this person. The prompt, the calculators' own range checks, the eval rows of step 8 and, for a regulated figure, a person carry the rest.
