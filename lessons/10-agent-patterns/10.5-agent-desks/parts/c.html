The arms side by side

--compare reads two or three reports of one split, one arm each, on the same rows, and refuses anything else: two reports of one arm, no report of arm B, two splits, or rows that differ. For each arm it prints the correct and authority rates with their Wilson intervals, the desk turns' rupees and model calls, p50 and p95 latency, and the turns in agent mode with their calculator results and refused calls. Then it pairs B against each other arm row by row, through evals/paired.py: Newcombe's interval for the difference, the exact McNemar p, and a verdict read against the margin written there before any run, %%MARGIN%% points, on %%MARGIN_REG%%.

@@paired@@ @@margin@@ %%CELL_COMPARE%% %%OUT_COMPARE%%

Reading it honestly. %%N_ROWS_CAP%% rows are few. With all %%N_ROWS%% right, a rate prints as "%%WILSON12%%": its 95% interval reaches down to %%WILSON12_LO%%. In the pairing, two arms right on every row give B minus the other +0.0 points, with an interval of [%%SAME_LO%%, %%SAME_HI%%]: "%%SAME_VERDICT%%". One row that B misses and the other arm gets moves it to [%%ONE_LO%%, %%ONE_HI%%]. Only when B is right on at least %%BETTER_W%% rows the other arm misses, and wrong on none it gets, does the interval clear the margin, and then it says B is better. The last line says why the verdict is indicative, never a ship decision: these are dev rows, not a test split people wrote, and B's exemplar index holds %%N_SEEN%% of these %%N_ROWS%% rows (jn-03 and the six lookups), so on them B's figures are an upper bound. What the paired lines do give you at %%N_ROWS_W%% rows is the rows the arms disagree on: read those answers.

The arms as an ensemble

evals/combine.py reads the saved runs as the members of an ensemble, at Rs 0. For each answer row it prints which members were right, by the route eval's own verdict. On a row where any member ran a calculator, the members vote: each proposes the (calculator, value) pairs its answer's calculations hold, which code returned and no model wrote, and the proposal more members made than any other wins. The answer of the first member that made it ships as it stands. A member with no calculation proposes nothing, and a tie abstains: the row goes to a person, since a regulated figure is not settled by a coin. Then come each member's rate, the rows where all, some or none were right, the vote beside the best single arm on the rows it voted on, and the figure check, desk_calc.unsupported(), on every member's answer.

@@vote@@

Do it: the combiners on scripted members, offline. --selftest runs on scripted members of fig-01, with no model call. Two members' calculators returned Rs 2,40,000; a third writes Rs 2,10,000 itself. With --layers 2 it adds an arbiter, which picks one member's answer from an enum of their names and ships it word for word, and two layers of a mixture-of-agents aggregator, each reading every answer of the layer before and writing a new one.

@@aggregate@@ %%CELL_SELFTEST%% %%OUT_SELFTEST%%

The vote and the arbiter only choose among the members' answers, so neither writes a figure of its own. The aggregator writes new text: this one averages the figures it reads and writes "%%AGG%%", a figure no member computed, and unsupported() flags it at every layer. That is the mixture-of-agents pattern's cost on a regulated figure: every layer is a model writing again, so its figures need the same check as an agent's, while the vote needs no model call at all. --layers goes up to %%LAYERS_MAX%%. On a lane the arbiter and the aggregator would be model calls of their own; the kit runs them scripted only.

Do it: the three saved runs, combined.

Arm C runs no calculator, so it proposes nothing, and on a row where B and Astar agree the vote ships B's answer. Then the members' rates, the vote beside the best single arm on the rows it voted on, and last the figures no calculation returned: a figure arm C's model wrote on a calculator row is what to expect there.

Design · Trade-offs

Why it is shaped this way, what it costs, and what the kit does not do yet

Five decisions, the price of agent mode, and the gaps.

  • The check is code; the rule is advice. Step 5 deleted the prompt's rule and the refusals stayed. A prompt makes a wrong call rarer, and a rarer wrong call saves a model call; the check makes one impossible to run.
  • Refused in words the model can act on. A refusal names the argument, the value, the source it lacks and what to do next: ask the person, search for the clause, search for the Code's section. The agent can put it right within the same %%MAXCALLS%% calls and Rs %%BUDGET%%.
  • The clause is the unit, and it has an edge. A number must be written in its source; nothing checks what it stands for there. Step 1's panel found %%N_EDGE%% such calls among its %%N_ACCEPT%% accepted ones. A finer unit, such as each figure of a clause labelled with what it is, would need the handbooks marked up for it.
  • A Code's figure needs the Code. A company that does not hold the Code on Social Security gets no gratuity figure: globex holds only the %%GLOBEX_HOLDS%%, so its desk cannot run gratuity_estimate.
  • Combine by vote, not by a writer. A vote over what code returned adds no model call and no new figure, and a tie goes to a person. An aggregator writes, so it can invent, as the selftest showed.

What agent mode costs. A direct answer is one rag-api answer: about Rs %%DIRECT_INR%% at the course's typical 2,000 tokens in and 300 out. A figure in agent mode is the classifier's call, about Rs %%L1_INR%% on %%L1_MODEL%%, then the agent's calls on %%MODEL%%: a search, a calculator, the answer, and one more after a refused call. At %%CALL_IN%% tokens in and %%CALL_OUT%% out, the passages and the tools included, a call is about Rs %%CALL_INR%%, so a figure costs about Rs %%AGENT_LO%% to %%AGENT_HI%%, %%RATIO_LO%% to %%RATIO_HI%% times a direct answer. The search for passages makes no model call; rag-api bills its embedding and ranking on their own lines, not counted here. Your reply's limits.cost_inr says what yours cost, and the cap holds every turn to Rs %%BUDGET%%. That is the trade the arms measure: arm B pays for agent mode only on a figure, arm Astar on every row, and arm C never; its figure rows show what that saving costs.

What the kit does not do yet
  • A test split people wrote: the arms set is %%N_ROWS%% model-drafted dev rows, so every verdict here is indicative.
  • Leaving a row's own group out of B's exemplar index on a lane: the index holds %%N_SEEN%% of the rows B is scored on.
  • Arm S, and fig-04, the notice-to-wages chain across two desks.
  • A smoke check that forces arm A*: smoke/smoke_desk.py does not.
  • A live arbiter or aggregator: evals/combine.py runs them scripted only.
  • The formal ship decision between the arms.
  • Reading a Desk thread back: make thread lists one and does not decode it.
  • Telling which of a clause's numbers an argument means, or which of two clauses covers the person: check_args() checks where a number is written, not what it stands for.
