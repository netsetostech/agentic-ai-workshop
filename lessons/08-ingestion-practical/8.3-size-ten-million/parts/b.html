Level 3 · The kit's code

The kit's numbers: the chunk, the request, the vector, the shard, the row

Every constant the arithmetic uses, read where the kit sets it.

Five things grow with the corpus: chunks, embedding requests, vectors, shards and Firestore rows. Each has its rule in one place in the kit. Read them in that order.

The chunk

@@chunk_consts@@

%%CHUNK_CHARS%% characters with %%OVERLAP%% of overlap. The comment's "~500 tokens per chunk" is the usual four characters a token; the kit's embedding batcher, below, counts more cautiously, at %%CPT%%. The cutting is _windows():

@@windows@@

A window runs %%CHUNK_CHARS%% characters, then moves back to the last full stop in its second half if it finds one, and the next window starts %%OVERLAP%% characters before that cut. So a long text becomes a little more than its length divided by %%CHUNK_CHARS%%, and the characters sent to the model are more than the characters in the document: every overlap is sent twice. Text with ## headings, which the kit's docstring calls a handbook, is cut by its headings first, one chunk per section, and a PDF's text page by page, so a page break also ends a chunk. Those two rules are why a short page or a short section costs a whole chunk: acme's contract is %%MSA_CHARS%% characters, less than one window, and %%MSA_CHUNKS%% chunks, the lines above its first heading and one for each of its %%MSA_HEADS%% sections.

The request

@@embed_consts@@

Two ceilings on one request, and the shared copy of the same function says where they come from:

@@embed_doc@@

The model takes at most %%MODEL_TEXTS%% texts and %%MODEL_TOKENS%% tokens in one request; the kit stays under both, at %%EMBED_BATCH%% texts and about %%EMBED_TOKENS%% tokens, counting a token as %%CPT%% characters. For full chunks, the token ceiling is the one that binds: a full %%CHUNK_CHARS%%-character chunk counts as %%TOK_CHUNK%% tokens, so one request carries %%PER_REQ_FULL%% of them, never %%EMBED_BATCH%%. Across the corpus, with its short chunks, a request carries %%PER_REQ_AVG%% on average. And the requests for one document go one after another:

@@embed_all@@

That loop matters at scale. The worker never sends two requests for the same document at once, so the only parallelism in embedding is the number of documents in flight, which step 8 reads.

The vector and the index

@@vector_config@@

%%DIMS%% numbers a vector, and the index stores each in 4 bytes, so Google's data size for your index is its vectors times %%VEC_BYTES%% bytes. That one number decides how many shards the index needs. The shard size is a property of the index, and the kit maps each size to the machine that serves it:

@@vector_machine@@

On your lane, %%LANE_SHARD%%; step 6 reads which. The deployed index, the part that answers queries and bills by the hour, has one replica, as both the minimum and the maximum:

@@vector_deployed@@

So the serving bill is a simple product: shards, times replicas (one), times the machine's hourly price, times the hours in a month. Ten million documents changes only the first factor.

The row

Every chunk the index holds is also a row in Firestore, with the chunk's text, its version flags and its vector. The rows are what the API searches when the index cannot answer:

@@row@@

The row carries the chunk's text and its vector, which Firestore stores at 8 bytes a number, twice the index's 4. And the rows are indexed for vector search eight ways, one per combination of filters the fallback uses:

@@fs_filters@@

Six indexes come from that map and two more are declared above it in the same file; every one of them ends in the document's name and the %%DIMS%%-number vector. Firestore bills its storage for the rows and for every index entry, and each of the %%FS_IDX_W%% entries holds the whole vector again. By Google's published size rules, the corpus's average chunk is at least %%CHUNK_B%% bytes in Firestore: %%ROW_B%% for the row and %%IDX_B%% for its index entries, about %%FS_TIMES%% times the %%VEC_BYTES%% bytes the same chunk counts for in Vector Search's data size. Step 8 prices it.

Measure the corpus with the kit's own chunker

The averages the arithmetic multiplies, from the kit's code on the kit's files, in a second, with no network.

The cell below loads every text document of the three tenants with shared/documind_corpus.py, the loader, chunker and batcher the notebooks share. They apply the worker's two rules, %%CHUNK_CHARS%% characters with %%OVERLAP%% of overlap and a section per ## heading, and the worker's two ceilings on a request. It counts, by document type, the characters, the chunks, the characters sent and the requests, then multiplies by %%N%%. It reads only files in your clone of the kit: nothing on the lane, nothing billed.

%%CELL_MEASURE%% %%OUT_MEASURE%%

Read the table from the bottom. %%N_DOCS%% documents make %%N_CHUNKS%% chunks and %%N_REQ%% requests, and the %%N_SENT%% characters sent are more than the %%N_CHARS%% in the documents, because every overlap travels twice. The %%SH_STAT_DOCS%% statutes dominate: %%STAT_CHUNKS%% of the %%N_CHUNKS%% chunks. A contract is a few chunks and a Code is hundreds, so the average, %%PER_DOC%% chunks a document, is a property of this mix, not of documents in general. That is why the calculator in step 1 offers four kinds: the statutes alone average %%SH_STAT_CHUNKS%% chunks, the handbooks %%SH_HAND_CHUNKS%%, the short documents %%SH_SHORT_CHUNKS%%. Before you size a real corpus, run this measurement on a sample of it; the kind of document moves the answer more than anything else on this page.

The last four lines are the straight lines at %%N%%: %%T_CHUNKS%% vectors, %%T_REQ%% requests, %%T_GIB%% GiB of dense index, and %%T_EMBED%% to embed it once at the online price. Lesson 8.2 changes the chunker and re-embeds; at ten million documents, every such change pays that last line again.
