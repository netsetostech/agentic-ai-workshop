Change a chunker, re-embed and reconcile: two lines in two files, one handbook re-issued, the ledger walked, the old image put back

The kit cuts every text document into chunks of up to %%KIT_CHARS%% characters, and it says so in two places: once in the ingest worker, which cuts what the lane indexes, and once in the module the notebooks, the Rs 0 lane and the offline checks share. This lesson changes the chunk size and its overlap to %%NEW_PAIR%% in both places, with a test that fails if only one is changed, and follows the change through a real deploy: a worker built under its own image tag, the handbook re-issued so the new rule cuts it, the counts of what was reused and what was embedded, the ledger walked against the bucket, the handbook's golden questions asked again, and the original image put back.

Two findings shape the practical. A chunk size of %%LONGEST%% characters or more changes nothing at all on the handbook, because no section in it is longer than that. And changing the rule re-cuts nothing that is already indexed: only a document the worker ingests after the deploy is cut by the new rule, which is why the handbook goes up again as new bytes.

Level 0 · Theory

What changing a chunker changes, and what it does not

A rule set in two files, a section rule ahead of the size, and an index that only learns the rule one document at a time.

A chunker is the code that cuts a document into the pieces that are embedded, stored and retrieved. Lesson 1.2 read the kit's: a handbook is cut at its ## headings, one chunk per section, and only a section longer than the chunk size is cut again, into overlapping windows that end on a sentence when there is one in the window's second half. Anything without headings is windows, page by page. Two numbers set the windows: CHUNK_CHARS, the most characters a window holds (%%KIT_CHARS%% today), and CHUNK_OVERLAP, how far back the next window starts (%%KIT_OV%%). Changing a chunker, in this kit, means changing that pair.

The pair is written down twice. services/ingest/main.py, the worker, cuts every document the lane indexes. shared/documind_corpus.py cuts the same documents for everything that runs outside the worker's image: the notebooks that seed a Colab lane, the Rs 0 lane on a laptop, the offline checks, and every lesson page that counts chunks when it is built. The image cannot import a notebook and a notebook cannot import the image, so each carries its own copy, the twins, and each says it follows the other. If only one is edited, the lane serves chunks that no offline count, check or estimate has ever seen, and nothing fails. The kit's twin test, commands/tests/test_twin_chunker.py, is what fails: it holds the two pairs equal and the two chunkers to the same chunks over the whole corpus.

The size is the second rule, not the first. Sections come first, so a size only matters for sections longer than it. The handbook has %%N_SECTIONS%% chunks today: a short preamble and %%N_HEADINGS%% sections. Of the sections, %%N_REAL_W%% are the clauses the course keeps asking about (%%REAL_LIST%%), none longer than %%SHORT_MAX%% characters. The other %%N_GEN%%, %%GEN_FIRST%% to %%GEN_LAST%%, are the handbook's generated filler, %%GEN_MIN%% to %%LONGEST%% characters each. The longest section of all is %%LONGEST%% characters, so any size of %%LONGEST%% or more leaves every section whole: the same chunks, the same hashes, the same vectors, nothing to embed. The cohort plan's "600 or more changes nothing" is true, and the exact line is %%LONGEST%%. At %%NEW_PAIR%%, every generated section is cut in two and every real clause stays whole: %%N400%% chunks, of which %%R400%% are the very texts the lane holds now.

Last, and easiest to miss: a new rule re-cuts nothing that is already indexed. The rows in Firestore and the vectors in the index were cut when each document was ingested, and the worker cuts again only when a document arrives. So the practical is a deploy (the rule), a re-issue (the one document you want re-cut, as bytes the lane has never seen), a reconcile (the ledger still names what the bucket holds), a gate (the golden questions about that document), and a way back. The kit has no command that re-cuts a whole corpus; INDEXING.md lists a full re-embed under "Not yet".

🗃

A coaching centre in Kota re-cuts its revision cards. Every chapter of every textbook is printed onto revision cards, each card filed in a catalogue students search. The press has two rules: a topic becomes one card, and a topic too long for a card is cut onto overlapping cards. Head office decides cards should be smaller. Three things follow. The short topics already fit, so their cards come out word for word the same and keep their place in the catalogue; only the long topics are re-cut, and the indexer bills only for those. Nothing already in the drawers changes: only a chapter sent to the press after the decision comes out on new cards, so the centre sends again the one chapter it wants re-cut. And the centre has two presses, one at head office and one at the branch that prints the practice sets: if only one press gets the new card size, the practice sets point at cards head office never printed. Every night a clerk checks the register of chapters against the shelves.

The two presses are the twins and the card size is the pair. The short topics are the handbook's %%N_REAL_W%% real clauses and its preamble, kept with their vectors; the long ones are the %%N_GEN%% generated sections, embedded again. Sending a chapter again is the re-issue. The night clerk is the reconcile: she counts chapters and their editions, not card sizes, so she finds nothing wrong either way, which step 8 shows.

↔ SWIPE

Two pairs of words are easy to confuse. A re-issue is new bytes under an old name, which the worker always processes, while the undo of lessons 1.6 and 1.7 is old bytes again, which the worker answers by flipping retired rows back with nothing cut and nothing embedded. And an image tag is a name that can be moved, while a revision is fixed: step 5 gives the edited worker a tag nobody else uses, so that step 9 can trust the original tag to mean the original image.
