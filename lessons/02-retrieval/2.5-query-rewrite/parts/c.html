Level 4 · Candidate

The stage on a candidate: QUERY_REWRITE=on, a forced failure, and the undo

A revision that serves nobody, one question with the stage on, the same question with a model that does not exist, the two log lines, and the variables taken back out.

Definition

QUERY_REWRITE is read once, at startup, and the validator refuses anything but off or on before the revision serves. Neither make target sets it. make deploy-services sets the API's whole environment with --set-env-vars, and the variable is not in its list; make candidate updates its own list with --update-env-vars, and the variable is not in that one either. A revision gets the stage from gcloud run services update --update-env-vars, as below, and that has two consequences. The next make deploy-services replaces the whole environment and turns the stage off again: a variable set by hand is a hotfix that a routine deploy drops, which lesson 11.7 drills. And --update-env-vars merges, so the variable would ride into the next candidate, make candidate's included, unless it is removed; the undo removes both. The pattern is lesson 2.2's: --no-traffic and a tag, a URL of its own, and the identity token's audience still the service's canonical URL.

What just happened

A revision with the stage on answered the paraphrase on its own URL while the live revision served everyone else. Its answer said which text found the pool, stages.search_query, and what the stage did, stages.rewrite; its citations came from a pool the reranker ordered against the person's question. With a model that does not exist, the same request answered 200 with fallback: the question was searched, exactly as with the stage off, and the failure cost a call's latency and nothing else. The two log lines named the outcomes, the error's class, the tokens and the cost, and no word of the question. Then the variables came out of the template and the tag went; the next candidate starts clean, and the next make deploy-services would have taken them out anyway.

Level 5 · Measure

The measurement: the rewrite arm against the lane, on the golden rows and the paraphrases

Lesson 2.2's harness with one arm added from outside the API, the paraphrases scored on their originals' anchors, the tally of the calls, and a reading that holds when there is no lift.

Definition

--rewrite adds one arm to evals/ablate.py, built beside the --arms keys and never as one of them: the lane's dense 20 then rerank 5, with the question rewritten first by the same shared/query_rewrite.py. The rewrite is what the Firestore rung searches and the question is what the reranker orders, as on /v1/query; a refused or failed rewrite searches the question, so the arm differs from the lane's only on the rows where a rewrite was accepted. Each distinct question gets one call, kept, so a retried row is not asked twice: %%GOLD_Q%% calls in the golden run, because two pairs of rows ask in the same words, and %%ALL_Q%% in the paraphrase run. --paraphrases adds the file's %%PARA%% same: true rows after the golden ones, each scored on the anchors of the row it rewords, and prints under each arm the golden share and the paraphrase share apart; the %%PARA_SAME_FALSE%% same: false rows ask something else and are never scored. So the paraphrase run re-scores the %%ANCH%% golden rows too: if you want one run only, run that one, since its split line carries the golden share. --limit cuts the golden rows first.

When the stage earns its call

Turn it on only where the measurement says so: the rewrite row ahead of the lane's on the paraphrases by at least one row's worth, and not behind it on the golden rows by as much. Then weigh what it costs per question: one %%MODEL%% call, about Rs %%PER_1000%% a thousand questions (%%PER_1000_SRC%%), and the call's milliseconds inside every retrieve_ms. Below that line, leave it off; config.py's default says the same. Even on, it serves only /v1/query, and the kit has no make switch for it yet: a revision carries it until the next make deploy-services.

What the runs cost

Each row of the ablation costs two embeddings, in paise, and one Ranking API request per arm, at the listed rate lesson 2.3 states, one US dollar per thousand requests, Rs %%RS_REQ%% each at %%USD_INR%% rupees to the dollar. Each rewrite call is priced here from the prompt's length through shared/prices.py; your tally lines print the real figure. An answer is priced by its tokens and printed on its own line; config.py's budgets cap one attempt at %%MAX_CONTEXT%% tokens in and %%MAX_ANSWER%% out, Rs %%ANSWER_MAX%% at %%GEN_MODEL%%'s list price.

What just happened

Two retrieval recipes ran over the same rows in the same run, and the only knob between them was the text the index was searched with. Each was scored by whether the anchor came back in the top five, not by whether an answer sounded right, so no model's prose could flatter either. Level 5 · Advanced

Change it: delete the prompt's literal rule and measure the guard; where the stage cannot go

Two defences against one failure, the first removed and the second measured on the rows it guards; then the routes, rows and deploys the stage never reaches.

Definition: two defences, one failure

A rewrite that loses a code or a figure is stopped twice. The prompt's second rule asks the model to copy every literal exactly as written; the guard refuses whatever the model sends back without one. Delete the rule and the guard is all that is left. The guard can refuse only a question that carries a literal: %%LIT_ROWS%% of the %%ANCH%% anchored golden rows, %%LIT_Q%% distinct questions, all of them acme's. The five rows that --limit 5 takes carry one literal between them, %%FIRST5_LIT%%'s "%%FIRST5_LITS%%", which is why this check takes a file of its own through --golden rather than a limit. The edit is to your clone of the kit only; the lane's image never sees it.
