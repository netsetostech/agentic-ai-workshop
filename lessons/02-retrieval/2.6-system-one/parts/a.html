Level 0 · Theory

Why decide before generating, and who may decide

A pool is not an answer, the kit's second refusal is its expensive one, three deciders can be held to one truth, and the third of them is not on the request path in this course.

Retrieval hands over a pool, and something must decide what to do with it. The golden set says what the right decision is for every row. Of its %%N_ROWS%% rows, %%N_ANS%% are questions the tenant's documents answer and %%N_UN%% are not. The %%N_REF_UN%% refusal rows ask for something no document of the tenant holds: ACME's sabbatical policy, its revenue in FY2024, the name of its CTO. The %%N_ISO_UN%% isolation rows ask one tenant what only another tenant's documents say. A system that answers one of those %%N_UN%% has written a false answer, and an isolation row answered is the worse kind, because the words came from somewhere the tenant's documents are not. A system that refuses one of the %%N_ANS%% has lost an answer someone needed. That makes the decision a classifier with a confusion matrix, and evals/decide.py scores it as one.

The kit decides twice today, and the second decision is the expensive one. When retrieval finds nothing, empty_pool_answer() writes the refusal in code: answerable false, backend none, no reranker, no model, Rs 0. When retrieval finds something, the reranker orders the pool and the generator reads it, and rule 3 of its system prompt lets it set answerable to false when the context does not contain the answer. A dense retriever always finds something: it returns the nearest neighbours of the question whether or not they answer it. So on your lane nearly every refusal is the model's, and the model's refusal is billed as a full call. For the sabbatical question, packed from the Rs 0 lane's five passages and counted the way the kit's packer counts, the prompt alone is about %%RF01_TOK%% tokens, before the model has thought about it. Lesson 3.5 compared the two refusals' envelopes. This lesson asks whether a cheaper decision could come first, and what it would cost in lost answers.

Three deciders, one pool. decide.py takes each golden row's pool from your lane's /v1/passages, which is /v1/query up to the reranker and nothing after it: the same door, roster, filters, guard, backend and rerank, then the passages, with no model. Three arms then decide on that pool. The rule is the kit's own code: the share of the question's information the pool carries, each content word weighted by its idf over the tenant's chunks, held against EVIDENCE_FLOOR, %%FLOOR%%. It is the gate the Rs 0 lane has always applied, and it costs nothing and takes microseconds. The generator is /v1/query itself, asked as the eval gate asks it (top_k %%GEN_TOPK%%), and its decision is the answerable it returns. Jev is TypeSafe AI's System One model. By TypeSafe's own page in October 2026, it returns a typed decision (a choice, a score, a value between 0 and 1) with a calibrated probability instead of text. Those are the vendor's claims, not the kit's measurements, and measuring them is what the third arm is for.

What is measured, and why every rate carries an interval. Answer rate is the share of the %%N_ANS%% answerable rows an arm answered; refusal recall is the share of the %%N_UN%% unanswerable rows it refused; accuracy is the share of all rows it decided right. Each is printed with its Wilson 95% interval, because %%N_UN%% rows is a small sample: even %%N_UN%% refusals out of %%N_UN%% leave a lower bound of %%W18%%. The live eval gate wants refusal recall of %%GATE%%. An arm that gives a score is also checked for calibration: does a score of 0.8 mean right four times in five? Every arm is timed and priced per decision. And an error or a skipped row is never a refusal: it is counted on its own line and left out of every rate.

Jev is not on the request path in this course. It is the kit's first model that is not Google's, so it arrives behind three guards. JEV_MODE is off by default and makes no call. recorded mode reads evals/system_one/recorded.jsonl, which does not exist: no recording ships until one is made against TypeSafe's API, and none is written by hand. live mode would send only the tenants whose data_region is any, %%ANY_T%% (%%N_ANY%% of the %%N_ROWS%% rows), with identifiers masked, and today it answers an error, because TypeSafe's adapter is a later pull request, 2.6-C, waiting on TypeSafe's documentation and terms. So wherever Jev's numbers would go, this page says Jev not available until the author's recording exists. The diagram shows where Jev would sit; in this course only decide.py calls it, offline.

The registry's scrutiny. A petition filed in a High Court goes first to the registry, which scrutinises it before any judge sees it: are the annexures there, is the court fee paid, is the affidavit sworn? A filing that lacks the papers to decide it is returned with objections, by a clerk, in minutes. A filing with no papers at all is not taken in at the counter. Everything else is listed, and the judge still rules: a listed matter can be dismissed after a full hearing, which has cost the court a morning. Scrutiny never decides the merits, and it is judged two ways: how many hopeless filings it stopped before they reached a bench, and how many sound ones it returned that deserved a hearing. Too strict, and litigants lose their day in court; too lax, and judges spend their mornings on filings that could never succeed. A court could even hand scrutiny to an agency outside the building, if its rules let the case files leave.

The empty filing is the empty pool, refused in code. The registry is the rule, or Jev, deciding before generation. The judge is the generator, whose rule 3 still refuses what it reads. Stopped hopeless filings are refusal recall; sound filings returned are lost answers. And whether the files may leave the building is a tenant's data_region.

The rule's floor, on the %%N_ROWS%% golden rows

What the bench is not

It is not your lane and it is not Jev. The Rs 0 lane ranks words, so a row's pool here is not the pool your lane's index and reranker return, and a question can carry every word of its answer without being answered. A recorded point is the author's lane, on that lane's pools, and it does not move with the floor because neither the generator nor Jev reads one. Nothing on this bench calls a model.
