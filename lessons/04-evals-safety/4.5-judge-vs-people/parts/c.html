Level 3 · The judge

The judge on the same rows: true-pass, false-pass and the cut

The judge's ratings of the frozen rows, held to your labels and to the author's, at a cut you choose.

Definition

Lesson 4.2's judge kept only means. judge.py --save-rows writes every row it rated beside its question, context and answer, with each metric's score and the judge's explanation, and the author's run of it rated the frozen rows once: evals/judge_set.ratings.jsonl, which ships with the kit once it is recorded. Until then the ratings are your lane's own: run step 6 first, whose second line is this step's comparison against your sheet, and read RUN=evals/reports/judge_set.ratings.jsonl into every make judge-agree here. make judge-agree, without PEOPLE, sets those ratings against one sheet, yours by default, and your labels are the reference. Groundedness is the judge's own 0 or 1. Its second metric is whichever of %%SECOND_NAMES%% the SDK names, scored 1 to 5, and it passes at CUT or more, %%CUT%% unless you say otherwise. For each rubric the kit prints the 2x2, the true-pass and false-pass rates with their intervals, raw agreement and kappa, the false-pass rate on constructed and natural rows apart, and the rows you and the judge differ on. A rating counts only where the judge rated the very answer you labelled, the same row_sha. The command exits 0 whatever the agreement: it measures the judge and gates nothing.

What just happened

Read the false-pass rate first: of the answers people failed, how many the judge would have waved through. A looser cut passes more rows, so the true-pass and false-pass rates can only rise together; a stricter one can only lower both. Kappa sums the trade up but hides which way the judge errs, so read the two rates. Constructed rows test groundedness on purpose: each carries one sentence its context does not hold, cited as if it did, so a judge that passes them is passing invented facts. Then set your two runs side by side. If the judge agrees with the author more than with you, read the rows where you and the author differ before you blame the judge.

Your lane's judge on the frozen rows: the ratings step 5 reads until the author's ship

The same rows through Vertex AI Evaluation on your project, then your judge's ratings against your labels.

Definition

While the kit has no ratings, this step is how the judge's ratings reach you, and step 5 reads them. Once the author's ship, they are one judge: the author's run of the Evaluation service, at one time, with one template. Yours may rate differently, because a judge is its template and its model, and both move. make judge-frozen sends the frozen rows, as frozen, to Vertex AI Evaluation from lesson 4.2's venv. --reuse loads them from the frozen set, the kit's evals/judge_set.json or your own evals/reports/judge_set.json, so nothing asks the API, and --no-enrich keeps each row's context as the readers saw it instead of reading the cited chunks again from Firestore. It costs two Evaluation requests a row, %%N_EVAL_FROZEN%% in all, in us-central1, and writes one Experiments run in documind-eval whose name starts api-frozen- and ends with the templates' hash, as in lesson 4.2. Your ratings go to evals/reports/judge_set.ratings.jsonl, so the author's file stays as it was.

Kappa by hand, and the judge's own reasons

Two standard-library cells over the files themselves, with no kit function between you and the count.

The first cell reads your sheet and the author's with Python's csv module, counts p_o and p_e by hand, and checks the result against the kit's cohen_kappa(). It is this lesson's arithmetic in a screenful, and it runs anywhere Python does.

%%CELL_HAND%% %%OUT_HAND%%

The second reads the judge's ratings file directly and prints each row where the judge and you disagree, with the judge's score and its own explanation. It is lesson 4.2's advice made concrete: where they disagree, read the row. It skips judge_agree.py's row_sha check, because both files come from the same frozen set.

%%CELL_REASONS%% %%OUT_REASONS%%
What just happened

The hand count matched cohen_kappa(), because kappa is nothing but counts: how many rows alike, and how many yes and no each reader gave. The judge's explanations are what a mean never shows. On a false pass, read whether the judge missed the unsupported sentence or read the context differently from you; on a false fail, whether it marked a refusal down for a figure it named. Neither cell writes anything.

Level 5 · Advanced

How the rows were chosen, why the judge still only advises, and what it costs

The freeze's rules and the author's freeze, the limits of twenty rows, and the rupees.

How the rows were chosen

The frozen set came from the author's lane: judge.py --save-rows on the live revision's answers to all %%N_GOLDEN%% golden rows, and on a gemini-3.1-flash-lite candidate's answers to the same rows, for more failures. build_judge_set.py --freeze then picked about %%ROWS%% rows, one per question, without looking at the judge's ratings: rows chosen by the judge's own verdict would bias every rate measured against it. A natural failure is found by the gate's own figure and refusal rules, run on the frozen answer. Natural failures seldom fail groundedness, so constructed rows make up that third, each with one of %%N_ADDED%% sentences, used only where its key phrase is absent from that row's context and answer.

@@freeze_rules@@ @@computed_fn@@ @@added@@ %%OUT_FREEZE%%

Why the judge still only advises

  • Twenty rows are a sample, not a calibration. With about %%THIRD%% failing rows on a rubric, even a judge that passes none of them has a false-pass rate of %%THIRD_0%%. A cut picked by reading the sweep on these same rows is also fitted to them: with no rows held out, it is a starting value, which is what the kit calls its default.
  • A new template is a new judge. Every run name carries the templates' hash. When it changes, the agreement measured here belongs to the old judge.
  • The labels measure; they never tune. No prompt, threshold or model on this page is changed from them.
  • Only computed checks block. Lesson 4.2's gate decides a release; judge_agree.py exits 0 whatever the agreement.
