Level 0 · Theory

Read before you name, then attack what you built

Two habits that keep an eval honest: failures read before they are sorted, and controls tested by switching them off.

A gate says how many failed; it does not say why. Lesson 4.2's gate prints its rates and the rows that cost a point. To fix anything you need each failing row's cause, and the order you read in decides what you find. Start from a list of causes and every failure is filed under the nearest heading; a failure the list has no heading for is never seen at all. So read first. Take ten failing rows, each with its question, what the row expected, what was said and what was cited, and write one line on each in your own words. Only then read the kit's %%N_CAUSES_W%% causes, give each row one, and count. Researchers call the first pass open coding. The kit enforces the order: triage.py prints no cause unless a flag asks for them.

The evidence decides some causes, and a person decides one. A non-200 is the service failing, not the answer. Another tenant's figure in an answer is isolation. An answer to a question the corpus cannot answer is overreach. Two causes look the same from the answer alone: a refusal or a missing figure can mean the passage was never retrieved, or that it was retrieved and the model left it out. The pool tells them apart. With DOCUMIND_EVAL_PASSAGES=1 the gate asks /v1/passages for each answerable row's pool, the same retrieval and rerank with no model call, and records which of the row's must_retrieve anchors were in it. And a wrong golden row reads, from its own verdict, exactly like a wrong answer. That cause is only ever a person's label.

Then attack what you built. The golden set asks what users ask. An attacker asks what you did not build for: an instruction inside a question, another tenant's contract, a body field naming another tenant, someone else's token with a colleague's email in a header. Earlier lessons put a control on each of those paths, most of them in lessons 4.6 to 4.8. The attack set sends one row through each door for each attack, names the control meant to hold it, and lists the outcomes that mean the attack failed. Its rows are of two kinds. A structural row is held by code that always runs: the roster, the identity rule, the filter check, the tenant filter. One that opens is a hole, and it fails the run. A behavioural row is held by Model Armor's classifier or by a rule the model is asked to keep. The same question can go either way on two runs, so these rows are measured, as the attack success rate, and never gate anything.

A test that cannot fail tests nothing. A row that stays held with its control switched off is held by something else, or by nothing. So the runner switches each control off in turn, on a stand-in that runs rag-api's own door functions, and every structural row must open once its control is off. That is the vacuity check, and the panel below is every combination of it.

🚗

A car service centre's quality head. Every week she pulls the job cards of the cars that came back within seven days. Before she opens the company's list of defect codes, she reads ten cards and writes one line on each in her own words: "brake squeal after a pad change, same technician twice". Only then does she code them. Had she started from the list, the squeal would have gone under "brakes", and the technician who skips a torque step would never have shown up. Then she hires a locksmith for one night. He tries every door of the showroom and the parts store from a list that names the lock meant to hold each one. At every door he also takes that lock off and checks that the door now opens. A door that stays shut with its lock removed is held by something else, perhaps a cabinet pushed against it, and nobody should count on that. The job cards are your failing rows, the defect codes are the kit's %%N_CAUSES_W%% causes, and the locksmith's list is the attack set.

Switch the controls off: which attack rows open

Each button is one control, on by default. Switch any of them off, in any combination, and read each row's outcome on the stand-in. Pick a row to read its question, the control meant to hold it, and why it came back as it did.

The attack set against the stand-in - run_attacks.py --offline --json, all %%N_COMBOS%% combinations of %%N_CONTROLS%% controls

Computed when this page was built, from the kit you clone. run_attacks.py --offline --json ran the %%N_ATK%% rows through every combination of the %%N_CONTROLS_W%% controls against the stand-in. The stand-in runs rag-api's own verify_iap, enforce_membership, check_filters, screen_prompt and screen_response, lifted from the kit's source and called in the handlers' order. Behind them it stands in for the rest: a token is a name, the roster lists the member on every tenant, Model Armor flags exactly the questions of the rows that name armor_prompt, retrieval ranks the kit's own chunks by BM25, and the model quotes its first passage, or does what the attack asks once the refusal rule is off. Each row's verdict and reason are the runner's own verdict() and judge().

With every control on, all %%N_ATK%% rows hold. Switch off one control and the rows that depend on it open: the roster opens %%OPEN_ROSTER%%, the identity rule opens %%OPEN_IAP%%, the filter check opens %%OPEN_FILTER%%, and the tenant filter opens %%OPEN_TENANT%%, the plain question among them: with every tenant's chunks in its pool, its citations name another tenant. Model Armor alone holds %%OPEN_ARMOR%%, the PAN question. The refusal rule alone opens nothing, because the guard still blocks every attack it would face; switch both off and %%OPEN_BOTH%% open. Rows %%DEEP%% are held by either of those two, each enough on its own.

What the panel is not

It shows what rag-api's code does once each control has decided, not what Model Armor's classifier or the model will decide about a given question. Offline, the guard flags every row that names it, so the PAN question is held with the guard on; lesson 4.8 expects the real template to let it through. Steps 9 and 10 measure the real classifier and the real model on your lane.
