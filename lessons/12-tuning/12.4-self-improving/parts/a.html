Level 0 · Theory

What makes self-improvement safe

A loop that learns from its own failures, with a person, the golden set and the gate around the one step a model takes.

Self-improvement, in production. An agent improves itself when its own failures feed a change to how it answers. Done carelessly, that loop is dangerous in three ways. A model that rewrites its own instructions can talk itself out of a rule. A loop that writes its own tests can always pass them. And a change that ships because it reads well has not been measured at all. The kit keeps the useful part, a model reading a failure and proposing a fix. It puts a person, the golden set and the gate around that step.

What may change, and how. Only rag-api's prompt, and only by adding to it. generator.py's SYSTEM holds %%OWN%% numbered rules. A prompt version may add rules after them, numbered from %%OWN1%%, and v3, the default, adds none. The versions live in services/rag-api/prompt_rules.json, and the file is append-only: each version's rules begin with the rules of the version before it, unchanged. Which version a revision answers under is a setting, PROMPT_VERSION. So one image carries every accepted version, and each revision picks one. /version and every usage row name the version that answered.

Who does what.

  • The model proposes one rule, on one line, and says why. It is sent the rules as they are served, the answer, and what a right answer holds. It writes nothing.
  • You write the question in your own words, and say what a right answer holds: the golden row's shape, its figure and its anchor. You read the proposal and decide whether to accept it. You paste the golden row.
  • The kit checks the row against the documents and the rule against SYSTEM's rules before anything is written. It refuses to write the evaluator at all.
  • The gate judges the new version on a revision with no traffic, with every golden row, the new one among them.
  • Traffic moves only through make promote-gated, and only on a green record.

What a rule may never do. It may never undo one of SYSTEM's rules. That means answering from outside the numbered context (rule 1), dropping the citations (rule 2), refusing less when the context does not hold the answer (rule 3), quoting a whole section (rule 5), or setting the rules aside. The kit refuses %%N_PHRASES%% phrases that say so, at Rs 0, matched as whole words. A rule worded past those phrases reaches the gate, and the gate measures what it does. refusal_rate must stay at or above %%REFUSAL_PCT%%% of the %%N_UNANS%% rows the documents cannot answer, so if %%N_RED%% of them are answered the gate is red. And each of required.json's %%N_REQ%% rows must pass on its own.

🏥

A hospital's morbidity and mortality meeting. Once a month a hospital's doctors meet about the cases that went wrong. The team that treated the patient presents the case in its own words, with the patient's name left out. Anyone in the room may propose a change to the ward's protocol. The head of the department decides whether it is adopted. An adopted change is added to the protocol as a new numbered item, signed and dated, and nobody rewrites the old items in place. The audit standards the hospital is judged by are not written by whoever proposed the change. A new item is tried on one ward before the hospital takes it up, and it is dropped if that ward's audit gets worse.

The case is the production row, and leaving out the patient's name is writing the question in your own words, never a user's. The proposal is make improve's rule, from a model or from you. The head of department is the reviewer. The new numbered item is v4's rule %%OWN1%%: append-only, with the reviewer and the date on it. The audit standards are the evaluator, which improve.py never writes. The trial ward is the candidate revision, the ward's audit is the gate, and taking the item up is make promote-gated. Dropping it is a git revert.

The loop, and what can stop it at each stage

Seven stages, each with the thing that can stop it beside it. Every value here is read from the kit when the page is built.

make improve, make prompt-candidate, make gate: the stages and their guards
1
The production row. A question answered wrongly on the UI. You write the row: the question in your own words, the answer, and what a right answer holds.
Stops it: a figure or an anchor that is not in the tenant's documents. The row is refused before any model call, at Rs 0, as build_golden.py would refuse it.
2
The proposal. make improve ROW= sends %%MODEL%%, on the global endpoint, the rules as served, the answer and what a right answer holds. It returns one rule and why, under a JSON schema, for about Rs %%RS_PROPOSE%%. Or you write your own with --rule, at Rs 0.
Stops it: prompt_rules.py. A rule is one line of at most %%MAX_WORDS%% words, with none of %%N_PHRASES%% phrases that undo SYSTEM's rules, neither of the prompt's labels, no rule number and no duplicate. A refused rule writes nothing.
3
The review. make improve REVIEWER=: you read the proposal and accept it. It becomes v4 in prompt_rules.json, with your name, the date and the proposer.
Stops it: you, by not accepting it. The proposer cannot accept its own rule, and a proposal made on a version that is no longer the latest is refused.
4
The golden row. Accept prints the R(...) row. You paste it into build_golden.py (lesson 4.1's method), then run build_golden.py and make eval.
Stops it: improve.py never writes the evaluator. One write() refuses its %%N_EVALUATOR%% paths. The builder checks the row again, and the offline gate checks the whole set.
5
The candidate. Commit, make build, then make prompt-candidate PROMPT_VERSION=v4: a revision of documind-api with no traffic, tagged candidate.
Stops it: a version prompt_rules.json does not list, before any gcloud call. An image that does not carry the version refuses to start.
6
The gate. make gate (lesson 11.2) asks all %%N_GATE%% golden rows of the exact candidate, for about Rs %%RS_GATE%%.
Stops it: %%N_TH%% thresholds, among them refusal_rate at %%REFUSAL_PCT%%% of the %%N_UNANS%% rows the documents cannot answer, and each of required.json's %%N_REQ%% rows.
7
Promote or roll back. Green: make promote-gated moves the traffic. Red: no promote, and a git revert of the commit that holds v4.
Stops it: gate-check refuses a red record, a record for another revision, or one judged by other rules (lesson 11.2).

The values are the kit's: prompt_rules.py's limits, improve.py's list of the evaluator, run_eval.py's thresholds over golden.jsonl's %%N_ROWS%% rows and the one you add, required.json, and the gate's rupees at lesson 11.2's typical answer of 2,000 tokens in and 300 out (Rs %%RS_ANSWER%% each).

What the panel is not

It checks the words, as the kit does, and cannot see what a rule means. The rule "worded past the phrases" passes here and in make improve alike, though it asks for fewer refusals. Only the gate measures what a rule does to the answers. Tick the dated source on v3 with a rule that passes, and you see something the kit does on purpose: two rules numbered %%OWN1%%. Step 12 says why.

↔ SWIPE

One distinction to hold: the check reads the words and the gate measures the answers. A rule the check refuses never costs a rupee. A rule the check passes has proved nothing yet, until the gate has asked every golden row of a revision that serves it.
