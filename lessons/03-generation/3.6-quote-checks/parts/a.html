Level 0 · Theory

A quote is a claim about one passage, and a prompt is a release

Why a quote is checked where it says it came from, what each place to look can and cannot see, and why a prompt's words carry a version.

A quote says where its words came from. A citation makes two claims: this passage supports the answer, and these are its words. resolve() turns the draft's [Source N] into a chunk id, a page and a score, one citation a chunk: a passage the draft lists twice keeps its first quote. The quote it copies as the model wrote it, cut to %%QUOTE_CIT%% characters, and when the model wrote an empty one it puts the passage's first %%QUOTE_CIT%% characters in its place. It does the same for a source the answer cites only in its prose, an [N] the draft's list leaves out: the citation is added, and its quote is the passage's opening. Nothing on the request path compares the words with the passage. Rule 5 of the prompt asks for the clause that answers, at most twenty-five words, and the schema describes the field as exact words from that source, but both are requests. So a citation's quote can be the passage's own words, real words from another passage under the wrong number, words the model wrote itself, or the passage's opening standing in for a quote nobody gave. Every caller of /v1/query receives it as evidence: the eval gate, the MCP server's retrieve tool, and the chat service's agents, which pass the citations on.

Where you look decides what you find. The live gate already looks for each quote somewhere: judge_citations() searches the tenant's whole corpus, the text mirrors under evals/corpus, and prints quote_support_rate as information, not a threshold, because a Doc AI extraction and a pypdf mirror hyphenate differently. The whole corpus is too generous and too strict at once. It passes real words under the wrong source, and it passes a passage's opening that resolve() put in for an empty quote or a bare [N], because both are somewhere in the corpus. It fails the generator's own repair, the three dots _draft() adds to a quote it cut, and any quote of a PDF whose hyphenation differs from the mirror's. quote_check() looks in one place: the passage the citation names, read back from /v1/passages, which runs the same retrieval and rerank as /v1/query and returns each passage's full text with no model call. That passage is the text the model read, so a quote that is not in it is the model's own doing. It gives one of %%N_VERDICTS_WORD%% verdicts: own, elsewhere, absent, substituted or unread. With DOCUMIND_EVAL_PASSAGES=1 the live gate reads each answerable row's pool again and prints quote_passage_rate beside the corpus rate, still as information.

A prompt is a release, and its version is a label. SYSTEM starts every prompt generate() sends, with DATED_RULE after it when a packed passage carries a date; the two are %%TEXT_CHARS%% characters. config.py names them %%LABEL%%, and the label goes wherever an answer goes: /version reports it, every usage row carries its version, the answer cache's scope includes it so that an answer made under one version is never served under another, and tenant_daily groups by it. A label is worth that only if it names one text. prompt_versions.json maps each label to the sha256 of its text, make prompt-version checks the code against it offline, and the same rule runs as a unit test on every push of the learner repository. Change one character and the sha256 changes; keep the label and the check fails, naming the next version and the line to register. A registered line never changes, so v3 names one text for good.

📰

A sub-editor, and the style guide's control number. A reporter files a story that quotes the council and cites "minute 14". The sub-editor opens minute 14, not the whole archive, and lays the quote over it. Minute 14's words: it stands. Minute 9's words under minute 14's number: a misattribution, true words in the wrong place, which a search of the whole archive would have passed. Words in no minute: the reporter's own sentence in quotation marks. "See the minutes" where the quote should be: nothing was quoted, whatever page is stapled to it. And a minute the archive could not produce that day is not judged, and not passed either.

The reporters write to a style guide with a control number on its cover. Change one word and it is a new issue with a new number, filed beside the old one and never written over it, and every story is stamped with the number of the issue it was written under. When readers complain about last Tuesday's stories, the stamp says which guide was in force. The sub-editor is quote_check(), the archive search is judge_citations(), the control number is the prompt's version, the filing cabinet is the registry, and the stamp is prompt_version on every usage row.

The quote check: a citation and its quote against six passages, by quote_check()'s rule

The bench holds a citation as /v1/query returns one: the passage it names and the quote it carries. It runs quote_check()'s steps in order against six passages of acme's handbook, cut by the kit's chunker and given the ids the ingest worker mints, and prints the verdict and the step that decided it. Beside each case is what judge_citations() said about the same quote over acme's whole corpus, %%CORPUS_CHARS%% characters, when this page was built. The cases are the nine that step 3 runs; type your own quote to see where it lands.

Quote check - quote_check() from evals/run_eval.py, as rules, on six handbook passages

The six passages stand in for the pool /v1/passages returns for lk-06's question; on your lane the reranker chooses them, and step 4 reads yours. The ids take the worker's form, acme:, the handbook's sha256, then the passage's position, as your lane's do. The rule is ported from quote_check() and checked against it on every case when this page was built; the corpus column of a typed quote is exact only when its words are in one of the seven passages this page holds.

The version strip: prompt_pin's rule, live, and where the label goes

The strip holds what make prompt-version reads: SYSTEM and DATED_RULE as generator.py sets them, prompt_version as config.py sets it, and the registry. It does not hold the registry's history in git, which make prompt-version also reads for the version it offers next (step 8). It hashes the text with the same sha256, prints what prompt_pin.py would print, and follows the label to the places the service writes it. The first five cases are the states step 7 walks through; three more are cases the kit's own test checks, and the last is one space.

Version strip - prompt_pin.py's check, the sha256 and the label's path, as rules

The sha256 is a JavaScript port, checked against Python's hashlib when this page was built, and the verdict lines are prompt_pin.check()'s, checked against the script itself on every case. The cache scope is semantic_cache.scope_of() for the UI's request, no filters and a top_k of 5. The strip reads nothing of your clone; make prompt-version does.

What the two benches are not

Neither calls a model or your lane. The quotes in the first are written by hand, and what a model quotes for a given pool is steps 4, 6 and 7's business. The second hashes the text in the page; whether your clone's files agree with your registry is what make prompt-version tells you, in step 5.

One distinction to hold: the quote check judges the model's words against the text it read, and the version check judges the code's text against its label. Neither needs a model call. The first is information the gate prints; the second fails the dry run on every push.
