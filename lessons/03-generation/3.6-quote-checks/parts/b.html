Definition

Four pieces of the kit decide what a quote can be and how it is judged. resolve() copies the draft's quote into the citation, or the passage's first %%QUOTE_CIT%% characters when the draft's is empty, and adds a citation with that opening for a source the answer cites only as an [N], [N, M] or [Source N] in its prose; a chunk gets one citation, its first quote. _draft() cuts an over-long quote to the contract's %%QUOTE_DRAFT%% characters and ends it with three dots. judge_citations() collapses the quote's whitespace, folds its case and looks for it in the tenant's corpus text: the gate's quote support, counted per cited row. quote_check() makes the same match against one passage, the one the citation names, after two steps of its own. A quote equal to the passage's first %%QUOTE_CIT%% characters is substituted, and a trailing ... is dropped before the match, because the document never wrote it.

Level 4 · The version

The prompt's version: the label, the text, its sha256 and the check

config.py's two fields, the places the label goes, the text it names, prompt_pin.py's rule and the registry, and make prompt-version for Rs 0.

Definition

config.py declares prompt_id and prompt_version, and the service never reads the prompt's text to label an answer. /version joins the two fields; usage_row() writes the version on every row it makes, query, stream and passages rows alike; the stream's last event carries the label; scope_of() puts the version in the answer cache's scope; and tenant_daily groups the rows by it. The text those labels stand for is generator.py's: generate() and generate_stream() start every prompt with SYSTEM followed by DATED_RULE, with nothing between, the rule only when a packed passage carries a date.

prompt_pin.py reads both strings and both fields by AST, without importing the API, hashes the %%TEXT_CHARS%% characters in UTF-8, and looks the label up in prompt_versions.json. It refuses to guess: if either prompt line in generate() or generate_stream() changes shape, it stops with exit 2 rather than hash a text the model may no longer receive. And it checks the code, not a service. A PROMPT_VERSION in a service's environment would override config.py's default; the deploy scripts set none, the one target that does, make prompt-candidate PROMPT_VERSION= (lesson 12.4), sets it on a no-traffic candidate revision, and step 7 checks that your service has none. Lesson 9.1's gateway backend also sends a JSON rule of its own, which is not hashed. And it binds one prompt: the query rewrite's of lesson 2.5 and the Desk router's of lesson 10.4 each carry a PROMPT_VERSION of their own, %%REWRITE_PV%% and %%ROUTER_PV%%, with no registry behind them, so the discipline of step 7 is theirs to keep by hand.
