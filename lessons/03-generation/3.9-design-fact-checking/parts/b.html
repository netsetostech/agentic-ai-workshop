Dimension 1 · Architecture

Architecture: the path a claim takes, station by station

Six stations, each the kit's own; why each one is there rather than its alternative; and the one check the request path lacks, run offline.

Definition

The design is one small service, the fact desk, and every station it calls is already in the kit. A claim arrives for one tenant, and the desk calls rag-api's /v1/passages with its own identity. That identity needs what the MCP server's account has today: roles/run.invoker on documind-api, which the loop in commands/lesson-12.2.sh grants, and a place on each tenant's roster, which make roster writes. A 2 draws this path; it also says why each station is there rather than the obvious alternative, with reasons that belong to this workload:

  • The door, rather than rules of the desk's own. The law's %%N_CLASSES%% classes are decided once, in front of every route into rag-api that takes a question. A desk with its own list would drift from the Desk's. A hit on /v1/passages comes back as passages: [] with the law's reply in answer, so the desk can tell a claim handed to a person from an empty pool.
  • /v1/passages, rather than /v1/query. One retrieval, used twice: the generator reads the passages, and the quote check reads them again, in full. A Citation carries a quote of at most 500 characters; a passage carries its whole chunk, which is what a quote has to be found in. An empty pool refuses before any generation is paid for. And retrieval keeps every rule rag-api keeps: the roster, the filters, the backend the tenant's data_region allows, the version rule, the rerank. The kit built this route for exactly this caller, one that writes its own answer.
  • The generator's contract, rather than free text. answerable is a field the model fills, not a reading of its prose; each citation is a number resolved against the passages the model saw; each carries the words it relied on. The desk keeps SYSTEM as it is, so its answers are the lane's answers.
  • The quote check, rather than trusting the quote. resolve() copies the model's quote as it came, and rule 5 is a request in the prompt that nothing on the request path enforces. quote_holds() is the kit's own rule, written for the tuning set: the helpdesk style of make_trainset.py drops every teacher pair whose quote does not hold in its chunk. It costs nothing, so it runs on every claim.
  • A sampled judge, beside the path, rather than none or a judge on it. The computed checks cannot see an answer that says more than its passage; the judge can, and explains. It reads one standing verdict in twenty, after the fact, because each claim it reads is %%N_METRICS_WORD%% autorater calls, and it never decides, because a model's score is not a verdict (lesson 4.2). It sends the cited passages to Vertex AI Evaluation in %%JUDGE_REGION%%, so the design samples only tenants whose data_region is any: the caution the kit itself takes before a Google Chat door may carry an in tenant's quotes (step 4).
  • A verdict row of the desk's own. One row per claim in usage_row()'s shape, with the verdict and any quote that failed beside it. No row the kit writes carries a verdict, and step 5 shows what that costs the alerts.

What just happened

The cache lost on the two large corpora at every scale, by a margin no likely price change closes: acme's corpus is %%ACME_X%% times the bound at 100k claims, Rs %%ACME_STORE%% a month just to keep cached, and Rs %%ACME_READ%% a claim to read. globex is the honest exception, small enough to cross at a few thousand claims a month if the retrieval path really sent its ceiling every time, which is why the line is a threshold and not a verdict. So the monitoring catches one of the four, and that one only in part. The rest are counted on rows nobody alerts on, or not counted at all, which is what the design's verdict row and its metrics are for. Two facts behind the table matter beyond this desk. First, terraform/quota.tf names %%N_NAMED%% alerts as intents, and only two are declared, both in alerts.tf: %%UNDECLARED%% are declared by nothing. Second, no policy counts documind-api's own errors. A reply the generator cannot parse is a 502, never a refusal (lesson 3.5): each one leaves a generation_unparsed line and no usage row, and no policy reads either. burn_rate, one of the three undeclared alerts, is the one that would have seen a run of them.

A rate needs claims to be a rate. The unanswerable rate is judged over windows of %%UNANS_MIN%% minutes. At 1k claims a month one such window holds %%WINDOW_1K%% claims on average, at 10k it holds %%WINDOW_10K%%, and at 100k about %%WINDOW_100K%%. A desk at the bottom of the range alerts on counts over a day; one at the top can alert on a share over half an hour.
