How to read it

Read the table a column at a time. pass is what the gate would make of these answers, with its interval: a difference between the two levels means something only where their intervals do not overlap, and with five runs a level that is rare. %%W55%% and %%W35%% overlap. distinct and cite sets are the variance a reader meets, and more wordings at one level is no worse if every one of them passes. thought and visible are output tokens an answer, both billed at the output rate: HIGH's extra thinking shows in the first, and the second is the answer's own length. Read what went out

The table cannot prove that 2.0 was sent: it prints each arm's settings, not its requests. The recorder's log can. Every call in the report carries its config as sent (the thinking level, the temperature, None when none was sent, and the cap) and what came back.

%%CELL_SENT%% %%OUT_SENT%%
How to read it

Two questions, two places. Was 2.0 sent? The log says so call by call: each edit call went out with a temperature of 2.0 and each kit call with none, all at thinking level %%KIT_LEVEL%% with a cap of %%CAP%%. A level or a temperature the model does not take comes back as the API's error, on that answer's row, as the bench's docstring says; an edit arm whose five answers came back took the value without an error. That is what "ignored" means here: neither refused nor, if Google's page is right, applied.

Did anything change? Compare the two arms' rows in the table. Two samples of the same thing differ by chance, and the kit arm at %%KIT_LEVEL%% has now been sampled three times on this page, in steps 6, 7 and 8, which shows how far it moves on its own. If the edit arm sits inside that spread, five runs cannot tell the temperature from chance, which is what Google's page for %%MODEL%% leads you to expect. If it sits far outside it, run after run, the comment in _call() no longer holds for your model: keep the reports, and raise it.
