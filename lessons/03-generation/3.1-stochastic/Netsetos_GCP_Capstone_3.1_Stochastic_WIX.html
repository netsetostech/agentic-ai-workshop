Level 0 · Theory

Why one prompt answers in different words, and what thinking buys

What can differ between two runs of one prompt, which difference the gate fails, what the bench holds fixed so that the model is all that is left, and what a thinking level and a temperature change.

Lesson B.5 explained the mechanism; this page measures it on the kit. A decoder writes one token at a time, each drawn from a probability distribution, so two runs of one prompt can draw differently and write different answers. B.5 built that loop in numpy, showed the knobs that reshape the distribution (temperature, top-k and top-p), quoted Google's model page saying that gemini-3.6-flash ignores all three, and sent a bare prompt five times. This page does not repeat any of it. It asks the kit's own generator, on your lane's own passages, and judges every answer the way the eval gate judges /v1/query.

Three things can differ between two runs, and only one of them is a failure. The wording: "45 days are encashed" and "encashment is capped at 45 days" are one answer to a reader. The citations: which of the passages the answer cites. The verdict: whether the answer carries what the golden row requires. Row jn-03 asks: I am an E3 leaving with 50 days of earned leave. How much is encashed and what notice do I serve? The gate requires 45, the cap on encashed leave in clause LV-07, and 60, the E3 notice period in clause NP-03, each as a figure on its own boundary, and citations that name acme's own, current documents. An answer that has all 50 days encashed has varied in the way that matters. The bench counts all three, per thinking level: distinct answers, distinct cite sets, and pass k of N.

What the bench holds fixed, so that the model is all that is left. A difference between two answers could come from retrieval (a different pool), from the prompt (a different text), or from the model. The bench removes the first two. It reads the row's pool once from /v1/passages, which runs the same door, retrieval and reranker as /v1/query and stops before the model, and every run answers from that one pool. The prompt is generate()'s own, built from that pool, and the bench prints the sha256 of its head, the rules every prompt starts with, so that you can see it is the text documind-rag@v3 names. What is left is the model: what it draws, and how long it thinks before it answers.

A thinking level is how long the model thinks before it answers, and you pay for it. A Gemini 3 model writes thought tokens before its answer. Nobody reads them, and they are billed at the output rate: Rs 0.64 per 1,000 on gemini-3.6-flash, 5 times the price of a prompt token. thinking_level sets how much it thinks: MINIMAL, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH. The kit sends LOW on every answer. jn-03 joins two clauses and needs one small calculation (50 days earned, at most 45 encashed), which is the kind of question where more thinking could change the verdict. The bench asks it five times at LOW and five at HIGH, and prints per level the passes, the thought tokens an answer and the rupees an answer, so that whether HIGH is worth it is read off one table. Five runs are a small sample: five passes out of five still has a Wilson lower bound of 56.6%, so every rate on this page is read with its interval.

A temperature the model ignores, sent on purpose. generator.py sends no temperature: its comment says gemini-3.6-flash ignores one, and lesson B.5 quotes the model page that says so. The bench can send one anyway. Its edit arm sends 2.0, the hottest value B.5's toy tried, beside the kit's own arm, and its recorder keeps the config each call went out with. So the run answers two questions apart: was 2.0 sent (the recorder's log), and did anything change (the table).

One recipe card, five cooks, one inspector at the pass. A restaurant kitchen hands five cooks the same recipe card and the same tray of ingredients, and asks each of them for the same thali. The five plates come back different: one cook puts the dal on the left, another adds coriander, a third folds the roti in half. None of that is a mistake. The inspector at the pass does not compare the plates with one another. She checks each plate against the order: the two items it names, in the quantities it names, made from this kitchen's tray. A plate with three rotis where the order said two goes back, however well it is laid out. Give the cooks ten minutes to plan before they start and the plates may come out more alike, or more often right; the planning is paid by the minute either way, and nobody eats it. And the stove has a knob marked "flame" that, on this model of stove, is not connected to anything. Turn it to full and the cooking does not change, though the order slip still records that you turned it.

The plating is the wording, and the items named are the citations. The inspector is run_eval.row_verdict(), and the order's two quantities are 45 and 60. The planning time is the thinking level, paid in thought tokens. The knob is the temperature on gemini-3.6-flash, and the order slip is the bench's recorder.

The gate's rule, answer by answer

Before the bench runs, see what its judge does with wording. The widget below is run_eval's own reading of a figure, normalise() and contains(), and the text half of row_verdict(), ported to the browser and checked against the kit on 51 cases when this page was built. Try an answer in the first box: a chip fills it with one, and the lines under it say what the gate makes of it. Then edit the answers below it and watch three things move: each verdict, the count of distinct answers, and pass k/5 with its interval.

The gate's rule on golden row jn-03: 45 and 60, each on its own boundary

A verdict here is the gate's for a reply whose citations are valid. A figure must stand on its own boundary once number words have become digits on both sides, so "sixty" is 60, "160" is not, and neither is "60.5". Distinct answers counts the texts that still differ once their spaces are collapsed, as the bench counts them, and pass k/5 carries its Wilson interval, as the bench prints it.

What the widget is not

It is the text half of the rule, not all of it. On your lane row_verdict() also checks every citation: that its chunk id and its object belong to acme, and that its version is one the ledger lists as current (judge_citations(), step 4). The widget takes the citations as valid, so an answer it passes could still fail on the lane for a citation; a recorded answer that did keeps the bench's own verdict until you edit it. It calls no model. And the "45 rupees, 60 hours" chip is there to show the rule's blind spot: it checks figures, not meaning. That is why the gate checks citations as well, and why lesson 4.2 adds a judge.

What just happened

Each answer came from generate_stream(): the same rules and the same thinking level, LOW, as /v1/query, and no temperature. It differs from the gate's request in two ways you will meet again. The stream has no response schema, so it writes free text rather than the JSON the gate reads, and the page asks for 5 passages where the gate asks for 6. Definition

Every /v1/query answer is one call of _call() in services/rag-api/generator.py. def sent(config) -> dict:
    """The knobs one request carried: its thinking level, its temperature (None when none was sent) and its
    max_output_tokens - from google-genai's GenerateContentConfig, or a dict of the same fields."""
    thinking = _get(config, "thinking_config")

def usage_of(r) -> dict:
    """What one reply billed, the thought tokens apart: the prompt (tokens_in, the cached ones among them), the visible
    output (candidates_token_count) and the thinking (thoughts_token_count, billed as output too), and why it stopped."""
    u = getattr(r, "usage_metadata", None)

    def n(key: str) -> int:
        return int(getattr(u, key, None) or 0)           # None from the SDK means "not this call", not 0 tokens

    cands = getattr(r, "candidates", None) or []
    fr = getattr(cands[0], "finish_reason", None) if cands else None
    finish = (getattr(fr, "name", None) or str(fr)) if fr is not None else ("UNKNOWN" if cands else "NO_CANDIDATES")
    return {"tokens_in": n("prompt_token_count"), "cached": n("cached_content_token_count"),
            "visible": n("candidates_token_count"), "thought": n("thoughts_token_count"), "finish": finish}


class Recorder:
    """generator._client for the bench's run. generate() reaches the model through _client_for(), which returns _client
    for a model name, so every call comes through generate_content() here: sent on with thinking_config at `level` and,
    when `temperature` is set, a temperature - everything else as generator._call built it - and kept in `calls` with
    the config as sent, the usage and the time it took."""

    def __init__(self, client, types_module):
        self.client, self.types = client, types_module
        self.level, self.temperature, self.calls = KIT_LEVEL, None, []

    @property
    def models(self):
        return self

    def configure(self, config):
        fields = {"thinking_config": self.types.ThinkingConfig(thinking_level=self.level)}
        if self.temperature is not None:
            fields["temperature"] = self.temperature
        if isinstance(config, dict):
            return {**config, **fields}
        return config.model_copy(update=fields)

    def generate_content(self, *, model, contents, config=None):
        config = self.configure(config)
        call = {"model": model, **sent(config)}
        self.calls.append(call)
        t0 = time.monotonic()
        try:
            r = self.client.models.generate_content(model=model, contents=contents, config=config)
        except Exception as e:
            call.update(ms=int((time.monotonic() - t0) * 1000), error=f"{type(e).__name__}: {str(e)[:160]}")
            raise
        call.update(ms=int((time.monotonic() - t0) * 1000), **usage_of(r))
        return r

def bench(gen, rows: list[dict], pools: dict, arm_list: list[dict], levels: list[str], runs: int, model: str, judge,
          out=print) -> list[dict]:
    """Every row, arm, level and run, interleaved run by run so a drift on the lane falls on every arm alike. One record
    an answer: its verdict, the calls as the recorder saw them, the rupees, and - when it came back - the answer, its
    citations and each quote's check against the row's pool. generator.SYSTEM and generator._client are the kit's again
    when this returns, whatever happened."""
    kit_system, kit_client = gen.SYSTEM, gen._client
    recorder = Recorder(kit_client, gen.types)
    answers: list[dict] = []
    gen._client = recorder
    try:
        for run in range(1, runs + 1):
            for row in rows:
                pool = pools[row["id"]]
                chunks = chunks_of(pool)
                for arm in arm_list:
                    gen.SYSTEM, recorder.temperature = arm["system"], arm["temperature"]
                    for level in levels:
                        recorder.level, recorder.calls = level, []
                        status, body = answer_one(gen, row["question"], chunks, model)
                        v = judge(row, status, body)
                        rec = {"id": row["id"], "arm": arm["name"], "level": level, "run": run, "status": status,
                               "outcome": v["outcome"], "pass": bool(v["pass"]), "why": v["why"], "calls": recorder.calls}
                        rec["cost_inr"] = sum(call_inr(c) for c in rec["calls"])
                        if v["outcome"] == "ok":
                            rec.update(answerable=body["answerable"], confidence=body["confidence"],
                                       answer=body["answer"][:ANSWER_CHARS], model=body.get("model"),
                                       cites=[{"chunk_id": c.get("chunk_id"), "source_uri": c.get("source_uri"),
                                               "quote": c.get("quote"), "check": run_eval.quote_check(c, pool)}
                                              for c in body["citations"]])

                        answers.append(rec)
                        out(answer_line(rec))
    finally:

def row_verdict(row: dict, status: int, body, current: set[str] | None = None,
                manifest: dict[str, set[str]] | None = None, corpus_text: str = "") -> dict:
    """live()'s judgment of one row, as a function of the reply alone (W2-A): the rule for every instrument that judges
    answers outside the gate - the prompt bench asks generate() itself, and must score a row exactly as the gate would.

    live() keeps its own copy of the rule, inline, because lessons quote its lines verbatim and a quoted line cannot
    move; tests/test_eval_rows.py holds the two equal over every golden row and every kind of reply. The inputs are what
    live() holds at that point: `current` the tenant's current shas (fetch_current_shas(); None skips the version
    check), `manifest` load_manifest_objects(), `corpus_text` the tenant's haystack() with its whitespace collapsed.

    Returns the record's outcome, pass and why, and the facts the rates count: answered (an answerable row the API
    answered), refused (an unanswerable row it refused), cited, valid (every citation the tenant's own and current;
    False when it answered without one), supported (quote support, when cited), kind_ok (when the row asks for a media
    kind), contained (every must_contain figure, when answered), and the leaks and stale lines live() lists. A fact
    that does not apply to the row is None."""
    v = {"outcome": "ok", "pass": False, "why": "", "answered": False, "refused": False, "cited": False, "valid": None,
         "supported": None, "kind_ok": None, "contained": None, "leaks": [], "stale": []}
    if status != 200:
        error = body.get("error") if isinstance(body, dict) else None
        v["outcome"] = "denied" if status in (401, 403) else "error"
        v["why"] = f"HTTP {status}" + (f" {error}" if error else "")
        return v
    problem = validate_body(body)
    if problem:
        v["outcome"], v["why"] = "malformed", problem
        return v

    if not row["answerable"]:
        if body["answerable"]:
            v["why"] = "answered, should refuse"
        else:
            v["refused"], v["pass"] = True, not v["why"]
        return v
    if not body["answerable"]:
        v["why"] = "refused"
        return v

    if v["cited"]:

def normalise(text: str) -> str:
    """Lower-case, thousands separators out, number words to digits - on BOTH sides of every
    live containment check, so "twelve weeks" and "12 weeks" are the same fact.

    The third live eval (7 Sept 2026) answered lk-23 with "300 or more" against a row that
    says "three hundred", and jn-08 with "12 weeks" against "twelve weeks": the right figure
    in the statute's own spelling, scored as wrong. must_contain measures the figure; this
    makes the check say so. Nothing else is relaxed - the words around the number must still
    match - and must_not_contain gets the same treatment, so a leak spelt "26 weeks" is
    caught where "twenty-six weeks" alone would have let it through.

    The second media eval (9 Sept 2026) added two more spellings of a right answer: "set-on and
    set-off" against a row that says "set on" (mm-02), and "5.2%" against "5.2 per cent" (mm-03,
    the figure the segment prompt had just learned to keep). A hyphen is a space and per cent is
    %, on both sides - the figure, not the typography."""
    t = text.lower().replace(",", "").replace("-", " ")
    for spelled in (" per cent", "per cent", " percent", "percent"):
        t = t.replace(spelled, "%")
    return _NUMWORDS.sub(lambda m: str(_to_int(m.group(0))), t)


def contains(text: str, want: str) -> bool:
    """normalise() on both sides, then the figure has to stand on its own: "60" is not in "160 days" or
    "3600", "8.33" is in "8.33%" and not in "18.33". The third live eval scored "300 or more" against
    "three hundred" as wrong; the review of 12 September found the opposite blind spot - eight rows carry a
    bare numeral that any larger number containing it satisfied."""
    w = normalise(want).strip()
    if not w:
        return False
    return re.search(r"(?<![\w.])" + re.escape(w) + r"(?![\w]|\.\d)", normalise(text)) is not None

def summarise(answers: list[dict], arm_names: list[str], levels: list[str]) -> list[dict]:
    """Per arm and level: answers asked, passed and come back (outcome ok); distinct answers and distinct citation sets,
    counted within each row across its runs and summed over the rows; the quote checks; the tokens and the rupees."""
    out = []
    for arm in arm_names:
        for level in levels:
            got = [a for a in answers if a["arm"] == arm and a["level"] == level]
            if not got:
                continue
            ok = [a for a in got if a["outcome"] == "ok"]
            by_row: dict[str, list[dict]] = {}
            for a in ok:
                by_row.setdefault(a["id"], []).append(a)
            quotes = Counter(c["check"] for a in ok for c in a["cites"])
            calls = [c for a in got for c in a["calls"]]
            out.append({"arm": arm, "level": level, "answers": len(got), "passed": sum(a["pass"] for a in got),
                        "ok": len(ok), "errors": len(got) - len(ok),
                        "distinct_answers": sum(len({flat(a["answer"]) for a in rs}) for rs in by_row.values()),
                        "distinct_citations": sum(len({frozenset(c["chunk_id"] for c in a["cites"]) for a in rs})
                                                  for rs in by_row.values()),
                        "quotes": {k: quotes.get(k, 0) for k in run_eval.QUOTE_VERDICTS},
                        "tokens_in": sum(c.get("tokens_in", 0) for c in calls),
                        "thought": sum(c.get("thought", 0) for c in calls),
                        "visible": sum(c.get("visible", 0) for c in calls),
                        "calls": len(calls),
                        "retried": sum(1 for a in got if len(a["calls"]) > 1 and a["calls"][0].get("finish") == "MAX_TOKENS"),
                        "fell_back": sum(1 for a in ok if a.get("model") != a["calls"][0]["model"]),
                        "cost_inr": sum(a["cost_inr"] for a in got)})
    return out
