Level 3 · The kit's code

What makes it keyless: the trust, the account and the workflow

wif.tf's provider and binding, sa.tf's account, documind-cd.yml's one permission, and the plan guard that keeps the trust where it is.

Definition

Four files make the pipeline keyless, and none of them holds a secret. wif.tf declares the pool, the provider with its mapping and condition, and the binding that lets a federated identity become the pipeline's account. sa.tf declares that account and what it may do. documind-cd.yml lets its jobs ask GitHub for a token, which Google's auth action exchanges. infrastructure.py keeps the trust from moving once it exists. Read them in that order: who is trusted, as whom, by which workflow, and what stops the trust from drifting.

The trust

@@wif_provider@@

The mapping copies four claims. sub becomes the subject, the name Google's audit log gives the federated identity (step 8), and three become attributes. The condition is the %%N_CLAUSES_W%% equalities the panel in step 1 evaluated, written with Terraform variables that your confirmed inputs fill. The issuer is GitHub's own, so only tokens GitHub signs are considered at all.

@@wif_binding@@

The binding grants %%BIND_ROLE%% on sa-documind-cicd to a principalSet: every identity in the pool whose attribute.repository_id is yours. The condition already pins the same id, so on your lane the binding never refuses a token the condition accepted. It is the second lock, for the day someone loosens the first, and the panel's middle trust showed what it still stops. The two outputs are the values make ci-vars reads in step 5.

@@wif_ref@@

deploy_ref defaults to %%TRUST_REF%%, and your confirmed inputs set it explicitly. The second resource lets the pipeline mint tokens as exactly %%N_TOKENS_W%% accounts, the UI's and the outsider's, because the live gate on the candidate asks as both. Lessons 4.2 and 4.3 ran that gate from your shell.

The account

@@sa_cicd@@ @@sa_roles@@

%%N_ROLES_CAP%% project roles: %%ROLES%%. The comments refuse two more: object admin on Cloud Storage, which at project scope would reach the uploads bucket of customer documents and the audit bucket, and log writing. The account may also act as %%N_ACTAS_W%% named accounts, itself included: %%ACTAS%%. They are granted one by one in cicd_actas, and lesson 0.3's step 4 shows that list and why it is never one grant on the project. (The comments carry the earlier course's lesson numbers.)

The workflow

@@cd_keyless@@ @@cd_steps@@

One permission makes the token possible: id-token: write. Without it GitHub mints nothing, and the auth step has nothing to exchange. The dry-run workflow does not ask for it, so its runs, on every push and pull request, never hold one. The auth step names the provider and the account through two repository variables, not secrets, because neither value grants anything to whoever reads it: only a token from your repository, on main, satisfies the provider. After it, make build and make release-candidate are the same targets you could run from your shell, run here as sa-documind-cicd. Lesson 4.3 read the two steps that follow: the check that the candidate answers as the tag, and the live gate.

The trust, once applied, does not move

@@infra_refuse@@

Every make plan reads the existing provider's condition back from the state, and refuses a plan that would change its condition, mapping, issuer or switch, or the binding's member. A prepare that names another repository, id or ref than the applied trust is refused too. That is why the repository you named at lesson 0.3's step 3 is the repository this lesson runs in.

What just happened

You read every line that makes the pipeline keyless, and there is no secret among them. The four values a run needs are public facts about your lane: the project, the region, the provider's full name and the account's email. What a run must be, not what it must hold, decides whether it gets in.

Level 4 · The dispatch

A build and a deploy from main, with no key

One run on your fork: the offline gate, a build, a candidate revision with no traffic, the live gate, then a person.

Definition

A dispatch on main with path=candidate runs three jobs. eval-gate runs the offline gate with no credential at all, as every push's dry run does. release-candidate needs it to pass, then signs in through the auth step, builds the API's image with make build, tagged with the run's commit, and puts it on a new revision of documind-api with no traffic and the tag candidate. It checks that the candidate answers as that commit, then runs the live gate against it: %%N_GOLDEN%% golden rows, as the roster member and as the outsider. promote needs a green release-candidate and the approval of the %%ENV_NAME%% environment's reviewer. Here you say no: lesson 11.2 judges exactly this candidate before anything moves traffic to it, and lesson 11.3 promotes.

The workflow's other path cuts a Cloud Deploy release from run-service.yaml and rolls it out over documind-api.
