Level 0 · Theory

What a stranger can check, and what only a defence can score

The rubric has two halves. The components exist or they do not; the dimensions are how well you can speak to what you built.

Eight components, each a thing in your fork. The capstone rubric, published on this page (the interactive below, and step 9), says every component must exist in the learner's fork and be checkable by a stranger in under ten minutes, because a component nobody can verify is a claim. A live URL behind IAP, your own corpus of %%PAGES_MIN%% pages or more, in a tenant other than %%KIT_TENANTS%%, an eval suite of %%GOLDEN_MIN%% golden rows or more with tenant isolation, one lane on top of the core, and four documents: ARCHITECTURE.md with the alternatives you rejected, COST-MODEL.md at 1k, 10k and 100k queries a month, FAILURE-MODES.md with four entries each proved by a script, and RUNBOOK.md with one recorded rollback. Each document starts from the kit's template in %%TEMPLATES%%/. A component is PASS or FAIL. One FAIL is an incomplete submission, and the defence does not run.

Five dimensions, scored only in the room. Architecture, failure modes, cost at 10×, evals, and what you would do next month: 0 to 3 each, %%POINTS%% points, %%PASS%% to pass, and no dimension at 0. The no-zero rule matters more than the total: a learner who cannot speak to failure modes at all has not built a production system, however good the architecture is. Two assessors score independently, and a split of more than %%SPLIT%% points goes to a third. Nothing in the kit scores a dimension. It cannot: a dimension is what you say when asked why.

The kit checks the first half. make capstone-check runs commands/capstone_check.py on a fork: by default the checkout you are in (FORK=.), or any directory you name. Offline it reads the fork's files at Rs 0 with no credential: the four documents, the corpus and the golden rows, the records make smoke-deployed and make gate leave in evals/reports/ (lessons 11.2 and 11.3), and it runs the fork's own evals/run_eval.py offline, as make eval runs it. With PROJECT= on make's command line (the PROJECT your shell exports does not count) it also reads the lane and changes nothing: gcloud describe and list calls from an allow-list of %%N_READS%%, two ID tokens minted as make eval-live mints them, and GETs on documind-api. It prints PASS or FAIL for each component with the evidence, or with what is missing and which lesson's command supplies it (for a missing document, its template), and exits 1 when any component fails.

🏢

An occupancy inspection, then a handover pack. Before a new office building opens, an inspector walks it with a checklist: the fire exits open, the sprinklers hold pressure, the lift has its certificate, the earthing reads under the limit. Each item is there or it is not. The inspector does not grade the architecture; a building with a locked fire exit does not open, however elegant its atrium. Only once every item passes does the owner sit down with the architect and the contractor and ask the hard questions: why this structure, what would fail first, what does it cost to run, what would you do differently. And on the day the building changes hands, the contractor leaves a pack: where the mains are, what the alarms watch, when the night systems switch off, who to call.

The eight components are the inspection. The five dimensions are the questions in the room. HANDOVER.md is the pack.

What the self-score is not

It is your reading of the rubric against your own defence, before the day. The assessors' reading is the one that counts, and the rubric is the only thing they score against. The defence is 90 minutes, two assessors, the live system on screen; the 60-minute peer mock the week before is practice, unscored, and the two are different events.

What the check will not tell you

make capstone-check says that a component exists and a stranger could verify it. It does not say the component is good. Three rejected options with six words of reason each pass component 5; whether those reasons are specific to your workload is dimension 1, and only the room scores it. Read a PASS as "the defence can run", not as "this is done".

One distinction to hold through the lesson: the check reads records, not the lane's history. A rollback counts when two successive make smoke-deployed runs in your log show the traffic leaving the revision the candidate tag names, with /version answered before and after. If you rolled back and never ran the smoke, the rollback happened and the check cannot see it.
