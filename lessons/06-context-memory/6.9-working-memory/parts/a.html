Level 0 · Theory

Every call resends the thread, and the window that caps it

The record and the call, the rule at every call, and what a call may carry.

The record and the call. The checkpointer keeps every message of a conversation, its thread, under a key of tenant, person and session (lessons 6.5 to 6.8). A model call is something else: the list of messages a brain sends Gemini for one step. Today the two are the same list. The LangGraph brain sends its system prompt and then the whole thread, and create_agent does the same for the LangChain brain. A turn that searches makes at least two calls, one that asks for the search and one that answers from it, and each sends every earlier question, answer and search result again. So each turn sends more than the one before. Over the twenty scripted turns of step 5, the forty calls send %%SUM_FULL%% tokens between them by the kit's estimate; with the window, %%SUM_WIN%%.

No trim today. Nothing in the chat service trims, windows or summarises a conversation's thread. The budget lesson 3.4 teaches has a line for history, which rag-api's own fit() sets to 0 because rag-api keeps none, and its comment says what the chat service does instead: its agents "resend whole threads, untrimmed". Step 3 measures that on your lane.

The window. services/chat/context_window.py is new in the kit. At every model call it takes the messages the brain would send and decides, on a copy, what the call is sent. The floor always goes whole: the system prompt and the newest turn, which is the person's latest message and every call and result since. Earlier turns follow, newest first, each kept whole while the earlier turns fit in CHAT_HISTORY_TOKENS, %%BUDGET%% tokens by default, the history line of lesson 3.4's budget. A kept turn is compacted: each search result becomes a short "cleared" note and the call that asked for it stays, so no call is sent without a result. A refusal stays as it was, so the model still reads why. The turns that do not fit leave the call, and one line at the top of the first kept message brings them forward: which turns, and as many of their questions as still fit, each with its turn's number. Code writes that line, not a model, so it costs nothing and invents nothing. A call is therefore at most its floor plus the budget, its ceiling, however long the thread grows. And the window writes nothing back: the checkpoint keeps every message.

The write rules. What a call carries from earlier turns follows the Desk's rules for a person's words (lessons 5.6 and 10.4). Every earlier question, kept or listed in the line, is masked, a checked card number becoming [card no.] and an Aadhaar number [Aadhaar], and then cut to %%PREV%% characters, the previous question the Desk keeps. The model's earlier answers are masked too. The newest question is masked and sent whole.

Not in the service yet. Nothing in the chat service imports the window, so every brain on your lane still sends the whole thread. A later change would put it in front of the brains' model, behind a switch that defaults to the whole thread. This lesson runs it where it is: in make window-check, offline at Rs 0, and in the author's run against Gemini.

📒

A bank passbook. The passbook holds every entry since the account was opened: that is the checkpoint. When a page fills, nobody copies the whole book onto the next page. The next page opens with a B/F line, brought forward, printed by the machine, standing for everything before it, and the newest entries follow in full. However old the account, a page has the same number of lines. The window is that page: the newest turns in full, a fixed room, and one line at the top. The machine prints the line, not a clerk, so it never says anything the book does not hold. And the page shows only the last digits of the account number: what is carried forward is masked.

What the panel is not

It is the kit's rule over one scripted conversation, not your lane: your lane's brains send the whole thread, and step 3 measures what that costs. The tokens are langchain-core's estimate, which leaves out the tool declarations every call also carries (lesson 6.1 measures those). Step 6 puts Gemini's own count beside it.
